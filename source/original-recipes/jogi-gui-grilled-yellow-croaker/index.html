Korean · Easy · Serves 3–4

**Stream: restaurant — the dried-fish tradition's fresh cousin.** Jogi — yellow croaker — is the fish of the Korean salted-and-dried tradition (the gulbi hung in rows at any market), and its fresh-grilled record runs that same idea on a home clock: a stronger salt cure than the other fish here, a longer rest, and a grill that concentrates rather than merely cooks. The gulbi tradition is the honest ancestor — this record is the fresh fish borrowing the dried fish's seasoning logic, and the table will recognize the flavor immediately.

Jogi Gui Grilled Yellow Croaker

Prep: 10 minutes, plus 1–2 hours salting · Cook: 12 minutes · Total: About 1 hour 25 minutes, mostly salting · Serves 3–4

Ingredients

The fish

  • 4 whole yellow croaker (about 200 g each), gutted, cleaned, scored twice on each side

The cure

  • 2 tsp coarse salt (about 10 g), worked into the scores and the cavity
  • A grind of black pepper

The finish and table

  • Neutral oil for the grate (about 10 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g), sliced scallion (about 10 g)
  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), kimchi (#B01), mu sangchae (about 100 g) — jogi runs salty-savory and wants the cold crunch

Instructions

  1. Oiled moderate grate, scored side down, 5 minutes undisturbed. The skin is thinner than mackerel's — it goes golden faster and wants a notch less heat. Checkpoint — golden skin at the scores, the flesh at the cuts turning opaque; the release is clean where the skin crisped.
  2. One flip, 4–5 minutes more, and jogi's doneness read is the same backbone test as the mackerel's (#SF07): at the deepest score, opaque at the bone and pulling away with a nudge. Checkpoint — a test flake from the thick shoulder: salty-savory, concentrated, firm but juicy — the flavor should sit somewhere between fresh fish and its dried cousin, which is exactly the record's register.
  3. Platter with sesame and scallion; the filet service runs the same two-fillets-one-skeleton as the mackerel record. Checkpoint — eating test: flake, rice, a strand of kimchi, and — the jogi-specific bite — a piece of mu sangchae's cold crunch with the salty fish. The table should reach for the rice bowl more often than the dip; this fish seasons itself.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The salted fish is literally the keeping method — 48 hours refrigerated, honestly better on day two. Cooked, 2 days; the flaked meat into rice porridge (juk) is the tradition's own leftover. - Variations: the mackerel flagship at the richer register (#SF07); hairtail for the skin crisp (#SF08); a light gochugaru dust in the cure for a warm undercurrent.
