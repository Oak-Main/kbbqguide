Kkakdugi Radish Kimchi

Korean · Medium · Makes about 1.5 kg

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the kimchi that matches the stew pots.** Kkakdugi is baechu kimchi's (#B01) radish sibling: the same paste, cubed Korean radish instead of cabbage, a faster ferment, and a crunch that survives weeks longer. At the grill table it is the bowl that loves the braised and stewed records — the jangjorim (#B20), the oily fish (#SF07) — because radish's clean, peppery snap cuts richness the way cabbage's leaves cannot. If you have made B01, you have already made this paste; this record is the cube law and the radish clock.

Quantities live in the recipe card.

The radish and the salt

The paste (the B01 family recipe)

- **Cube evenly — the cube is the recipe's clock.** 2.5 cm cubes salt and ferment at the same rate; a batch mixed with half-cubes and whole chunks ferments unevenly (the small cubes go sour while the large ones stay raw). Weigh your knife discipline here, not your ingredients. - **Salt the cubes 30 minutes**, loose in a bowl, turning once. The radish weeps a brine and its corners soften a half-step. Save 2 tablespoons of the wept brine for the paste; drain the rest. - Make the paste while the cubes drain: everything above whisked to a rust slurry, scallions last.

Kkakdugi Radish Kimchi

Prep: 45 minutes, hands-on · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 45 minutes, then 1–2 days fermenting, then the fridge · Makes about 1.5 kg

Ingredients

The radish and the salt

  • 1.5 kg Korean radish (mu) or daikon, peeled, cut into 2.5 cm cubes
  • 3 tbsp coarse salt (about 45 g)

The paste (the B01 family recipe)

  • 1/3 cup gochugaru (about 35 g)
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce (about 45 ml)
  • 1 tbsp salted shrimp (about 15 g), optional
  • 6 cloves garlic (about 24 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 15 g), grated
  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 100 g), grated
  • 1 tsp sugar (about 4 g)
  • 4 scallions (about 60 g), cut into batons
  • 2 tbsp water (30 ml), or the drained brine

Instructions

  1. The drained cubes and the paste folded together with your hands until every cube wears a coat and the bowl runs deep red-orange. Checkpoint — taste a pasted cube raw: salty, savory-hot, the radish's sweet pepper under it. This is day zero — the flavor that ferments into the bowl.
  2. Pack into a clean jar with 3 cm of headspace, pressed to squeeze out air, weight on top, loose lid — B01's entire packing law, unchanged. Checkpoint — brine rises over the cubes within a day (top up with the saved brine or salted water if it lags — under-liquid is the safety architecture, same as the cabbage).
  3. Ferment at cool room temperature 1–2 days (radish runs faster than cabbage — its sugars are more available) until the brine fizzes faintly and the sourness arrives, then refrigerate. Checkpoint — day-two taste: the cube snaps audibly, the paste's heat has rounded, a clean sour line on the finish. Kkakdugi's window from young-crunchy to complex-sour is weeks in the fridge, and its snap outlives cabbage's by a wide margin.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Refrigerated, honestly good a month — radish's crunch holds longer than cabbage's. The brine keeps serving (see Note) until the jar is empty. - Variations: the cabbage parent (#B01); cubed Asian pear folded in at packing (the palace style, sweet bursts); the same paste on cucumber runs #B03 at its faster clock.
