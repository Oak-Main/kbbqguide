Oi Sobagi Cucumber Kimchi

Korean · Easy · Makes about 800 g (a small jar, eaten within the week)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the summer day's kimchi.** Oi sobagi is the kimchi of one afternoon: cucumbers stuffed with the kimchi paste, ready by dinner and gone by the weekend. Where the cabbage jar (#B01) runs a month's arc, cucumber kimchi runs three days — it has no long-ferment ambition, because cucumber's water works fast in both directions. This is the bowl to make the morning of a summer grill, the cold crunch against the pork records' (#M10, #M11) richness.

The cucumbers and salt

The stuffing paste

- **Cut the pockets, not through.** Each cucumber is quartered lengthwise the careful way: knife down from the top, stopping 2 cm short of the end, twice in a cross — so each cucumber opens into four connected fingers, a pocket for the stuffing. The uncut end holds it all together. - **Salt 30 minutes** in cold water with the salt dissolved, weighted lightly; the cucumbers bend a half-step and weep. Rinse and drain well — a wet cucumber waters the paste down to a sauce.

Oi Sobagi Cucumber Kimchi

Prep: 30 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 30 minutes, best 6–24 hours later · Makes about 800 g (a small jar, eaten within the week)

Ingredients

The cucumbers and salt

  • 6 small Korean (pickling) cucumbers or kirby (about 900 g)
  • 2 tbsp coarse salt (about 30 g)

The stuffing paste

  • 3 tbsp gochugaru (about 18 g)
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce (30 ml)
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 10 g), grated
  • 1 tsp sugar (about 4 g)
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g) and a handful of Asian chives (about 20 g), cut small
  • 100 g Korean radish or carrot, fine matchsticks
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)

Instructions

  1. Build the stuffing: gochugaru, fish sauce, garlic, ginger, sugar worked to a paste, then the scallion, chives, radish matchsticks and sesame folded through so it holds together as a coarse stuffing rather than a slurry. Checkpoint — taste a matchstick: full kimchi register, slightly stronger than you want to eat — the cucumber will dilute it over the hours.
  2. Open each cucumber's fingers and press the stuffing into the pocket and between every finger, a generous teaspoon's worth; close, roll lightly in the bowl's remaining paste. Checkpoint — each cucumber is visibly stuffed, red in the seams, green at the shoulders.
  3. Pack snugly in a container, lid on, refrigerate. Ready at 6 hours, best at 24. Checkpoint — the day-of taste: cold snap, the stuffing's salt-heat in the seams, a barely-started sourness. By day two the sourness is present; by day three the cucumbers are softening — the bowl's honest end.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Refrigerated, at its best days 1–2, over by day 4 — this is a small-batch, same-week bowl by design. - Variations: the month-scale jars (#B01, #B02) for the long arc; the instant unfermented cucumber lane is oi muchim (#B07); the stuffing doubles inside whole radish wedges for a same-day kkakdugi cousin.
