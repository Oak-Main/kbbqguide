**Stream: restaurant, the instant-toss clock.** Jumulleok means "hand-mixed" — cubed steak tossed in seasoning minutes before it hits the fire, not hours. It is the Gwangyang school of timing (#M08's dry-grate cousin logic, applied to cubes): the juice stays inside the meat because the marinade never had time to pull it out. Of everything on this site, this is the fastest route from the butcher to the table — ten minutes of prep, a hot grate, and the seasoning bowl right at the grill so the next batch can be tossed while the first one cooks. Who cooks what: the seasoning bowl lives at the grill boss's station. Toss, grill, repeat.

Jumulleok Sesame-Garlic Steak Bites

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 20 minutes · Serves 3–4

Ingredients

The beef

  • 700 g sirloin or tenderloin, cut into 3 cm cubes (refrigerated until the toss — food safety is the one place this record is strict)

The toss

  • 1.5 tbsp toasted sesame oil (about 22 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp soy sauce (about 22 ml)
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), grated
  • 1/2 tsp coarse salt (about 3 g)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 500 g cooked), gireumjang (#SA02) for extra dipping, kimchi (#B01), lettuce ssam

Instructions

  1. Toss the first batch: meat into the bowl, hands or tongs, thirty seconds of turning until every cube wears the mix. Onto the hot grate with a finger's width between cubes — crowded cubes steam gray. Checkpoint — the cubes should hit with an aggressive sizzle and the sesame-garlic smell should rise immediately. No sizzle, no sear — pull the batch back and let the grate recover.
  2. Grill 90 seconds undisturbed, turn each cube to a new face, another 90 seconds, then roll to the last faces for 30–60 seconds. Total cook: about 4 minutes for medium at 3 cm. Pull when the faces are browned and the cube feels softly springy at a finger press. Checkpoint — cut one open at the grill station: a thin browned crust and a rosy interior. Gray to the center means the grate was too cool or the cubes too big — 3 cm is the ceiling.
  3. Sesame seeds over, warm platter, straight to the table. Toss the next batch while the table eats this one — the rhythm of the record is toss, grill, eat, repeat, and the meat is at its best in the first minute off the fire. Checkpoint — eating test: crust, juice, sesame. The center should taste of beef and black pepper more than soy — if it reads salty, the soy ran a teaspoon heavy; the toss is measured in grams for a reason.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- This is a cook-and-eat record; the toss doesn't hold. Leftover cooked cubes keep 2 days and make the best fried rice in this whole category — the sesame-garlic crust re-toasts beautifully in a hot pan. - Variations: a teaspoon of gochugaru in the toss for slow heat; butter and a splash of soy in the last minute for a glaze finish; the same toss on a whole sliced skirt steak, grilled as sheets, is the fastest cousin of #M01 without the slurry.
