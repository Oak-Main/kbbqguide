Mushroom-Soy Beef Bulgogi

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

**Stream: contemporary.** Same Seoul-lane architecture as the pear classic (#M01) — thin shavings, sweet-soy marinade, fast hot grill — but the sweetness is trimmed back and the savory dial is cranked with dried mushrooms. It is the bulgogi for the person who loves the genre but wants less sugar and more depth: shiitake powder and a splash of its soaking water turn plain soy into something that tastes like it simmered all day. Twenty minutes of that marinate does real work.

Mushroom-Soy Beef Bulgogi

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 2 hours marinating · Cook: 10 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The beef

  • 700 g sirloin or chuck eye, sliced 1–2 mm against the grain (bulgogi-slice)

The marinade

  • 6 dried shiitake mushrooms (about 20 g)
  • 120 ml hot water, for soaking
  • 80 ml soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp honey or brown sugar (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp rice wine or mirin (15 ml)
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 15 g), grated
  • 1/2 onion (about 75 g), grated
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), sliced king oyster or fresh shiitake (100 g) for the moat, kimchi (#B01), sesame seeds (about 4 g) and sliced scallion (about 15 g)

Instructions

  1. Whisk the marinade: strained soaking water (about 60 ml), soy, honey, rice wine, garlic, ginger, grated onion, sesame oil, pepper, and the mushroom paste. Add the beef and toss by hand until every shaving is coated. Press flat, cover, refrigerate 2 hours. Checkpoint — taste the marinade on a fingertip before the beef goes in: salty first, savory-mushroom second, sweetness clearly behind both. If it tastes as sweet as #M01's slurry, the balance drifted — this lane keeps the sugar in the back seat.
  2. Grill hot and in batches — charcoal or a smoking cast-iron pan. Single loose layer; the shavings take 60–90 seconds a side. Flip when the edges weep and color, pull when the raw red is just gone. Toss the sliced fresh and rehydrated mushrooms at the pan's edge in the rendering juice; they drink the marinade the meat leaves behind. Checkpoint — eating test off the grill: the edge should be savory-brown, the middle juicy, and the aftertaste should be mushroom and soy, not sugar. The mushrooms should be glossy and nearly meaty.
  3. Serve over rice with the pan juice, scallion and sesame over everything. Checkpoint — the bowl test: juice over rice first, meat and mushrooms over, and the whole bowl should read as a deeper, more savory version of the classic — unmistakably the same family, unmistakably not the same dish.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCauseFix
Marinade grittyUnstrained soak or unground capsStrain the soak; paste only two caps
Tastes just like regular bulgogiSkimpy mushroom pasteTwo full caps ground in, not one
Meat steamed grayPan too cool or batch piledReheat pan between small batches
Too saltySoaking water plus soy doubled upUse low-sodium soy or dilute soak 50/50

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated beef keeps 24 hours refrigerated. Cooked, it keeps 3 days and makes an excellent bibimbap topping or mushroom-fried-rice base — the savoriness survives reheating better than the pear version. - Variations: add a teaspoon of gochujang for a faint spice line toward #M13; swap beef for chicken thighs (#M16 architecture); fold the leftover soaking water into doenjang jjigae instead of water.
