**Stream: heritage — the meal's closing cup.** Sikhye — fermented rice punch, the faintly sparkling, gently sweet, malty-cold drink with rice grains drifting at the bottom of the cup — is how the traditional Korean table closed a heavy meal: not dessert's weight but a cold, alive, barely-sweet digestif that the holiday table poured after the meat and the fried everything. It closes this site's ledger the same way. Sikhye Sweet Rice Punch

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 4–6 hours incubating · Cook: 1 hour · Total: About 6 hours, mostly untended · Makes about 1.5 liters (serves 6)

Ingredients

The ferment

  • 200 g short-grain rice (or sweet rice, the deeper traditional build), cooked soft with a little extra water (a loose pot, not a fluffy one)
  • 200 g malt powder (yeotkireum, Korean barley malt — the enzyme source, from any Korean market), mixed with 1.5 liters warm water and rested 30 minutes, then strained through cloth or fine sieve, pressing the liquid out
  • 60 g sugar, or to taste
  • A pinch of ginger (about 2 g), a few pine nuts (about 8 g) and 2 dried jujubes (about 10 g) for serving cups, optional

Instructions

  1. The strained malt liquid poured over the cooked rice in a heavy pot, at a temperature your wrist reads as warm-not-hot (about 55 °C — the enzymes' working range; much above 65 °C they die), and held there: pot lid on, pot wrapped in a towel, in the warmest room or a 60 °C oven's residual heat, 4–6 hours. Checkpoint — at the hours' end, the evidence of conversion is unmistakable: the grains float to the top, hollowed and translucent, and a taste of the liquid is sweet — the starch became maltose while you were elsewhere. No floating, no sweetness: the hold ran too cool — reheat gently to warm and give it two more hours.
  2. **Boil briefly to stop the ferment:** lift the floated grains out (rinse them, keep them — they are the drink's drifting rice), bring the liquid to a bare boil with the sugar, 5 minutes, skim, and chill thoroughly. Checkpoint — the cooled taste: lightly sweet, malty, faintly sparkling-fresh on the tongue, none of the raw-malt's edge. Sweetness dials up or down with sugar at this stage — sikhye runs gently sweet, a closing drink, not a soda.
  3. Serve ice-cold in small cups, a spoon of the reserved rice grains in each, a pine nut and a jujube floating. Checkpoint — the drinking test: cold, malty-sweet, the grains eaten from the cup's bottom with the spoon after the liquid — the drink and its bite, the meal's last act. It should feel like the table exhaling.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 4–5 days refrigerated (the boil and the cold hold it; the ferment's clock slows, never stops — drink it young). The grains keep separately in a little of the liquid. Cans of sikhye are the market's honest shortcut for the week the afternoon won't cooperate. - Variations: the yeot-sweetened winter build; a drop of yuja (#F09's jar) per cup for the citrus fork; the floating grains over shaved ice, sikhye-bingsu, the summer café's brilliant closing move.
