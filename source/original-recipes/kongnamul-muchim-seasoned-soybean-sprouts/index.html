Korean · Easy · Serves 4–6 as banchan

**Stream: heritage — the everyday namul.** Soybean sprouts are the banchan that appears at nearly every Korean meal, including the grill table's — crunchy, nutty, cheap, and ready in fifteen minutes. The whole technique is one law: cook them uncovered so the bean's raw, grassy edge cooks off with the steam, then dress them while warm so they drink the sesame and garlic. This is also the parent recipe for the whole muchim/namul family on this site (#B06, #B17): blanch, shock, season is the pattern.

Kongnamul Muchim Seasoned Soybean Sprouts

Prep: 5 minutes · Cook: 6 minutes · Total: About 15 minutes · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Ingredients

The sprouts

  • 500 g soybean sprouts (kongnamul, the yellow-beaned ones — mung sprouts are a different, softer thing), rinsed, loose hulls and sad stragglers picked out

The cook

  • Water to barely cover (about 1 liter), 1 tsp salt (about 5 g)

The seasoning

  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), sliced
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • A pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional color
  • 1 tsp fish sauce or soy sauce (about 5 ml), optional depth

Instructions

  1. Salted water at a full boil, sprouts in, **uncovered** — the law of this record. Six minutes at a steady boil, stirred once. Checkpoint — a test sprout: the tail is fully tender (no string) and the bean is just soft with the faintest bite, and the raw-grassy smell from the pot's first minute has cooked off entirely — that smell leaving is exactly what the uncovered boil lets happen.
  2. Drain well — really well, a full minute in the colander, shaking — and turn the hot sprouts into the seasoning bowl while they still steam. Checkpoint — the residual heat is doing the garlic's cooking: thirty seconds after tossing, the bowl smells nutty-savory, not sharp-raw.
  3. Toss with everything, taste, adjust — the banchan should read sesame-first, salt behind, a clean crunch. Checkpoint — the eating test with rice and any grilled meat from this site's M-list: the sprouts reset the palate between rich bites. That is the banchan's job description, and this bowl does it as well as anything on the table.

Troubleshooting

Limp tailsOvercooked or sprouts oldSix minutes at a rolling boil; buy firm, white tails
Sharp raw garlicDressed after coolingDress while steaming — the heat cooks it

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2–3 days refrigerated and holds crunch better than most banchan; refresh with a few drops of sesame oil at serving. - Variations: a spoon of gochugaru for the red build; the spinach (#B06) and bellflower (#B17) records run this same blanch-and-dress pattern; day-after sprouts folded into bibimbap or fried rice.
