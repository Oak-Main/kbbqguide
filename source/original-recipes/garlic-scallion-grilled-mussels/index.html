**Stream: restaurant.** The mussel record runs the grill's simplest shellfish law: heat opens the shell, the sauce rides in on the liquor, and the pot's famous question — open or discard — is the only safety grammar to learn. Garlic, scallion, butter, and a gochujang thread turn the mussel's own brine into its sauce; the pan is the platter; the bread or rice does the rest.

Garlic-Scallion Grilled Mussels

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 20 minutes · Serves 4

Ingredients

The mussels

  • 1 kg fresh mussels, scrubbed, beards removed, closed (tap-test any gapers: a live one closes; an open non-closer is discarded)

The sauce

  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (about 30 g)
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), sliced thin, not grated — slices toast sweet
  • 4 scallions (about 60 g), sliced, whites and greens separated
  • 1 tbsp gochujang (about 20 g), optional but this record's signature
  • 1 tbsp mirin or water (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)

The table

  • Crusty bread (about 300 g) or steamed rice (about 400 g cooked), lemon wedges, an empty bowl for shells at the table's center

Instructions

  1. Mussels into the warm pan with the toasted base and a splash of mirin, lid on, high heat under now — they steam in their own liquor, 3–5 minutes, one hard shake at the halfway mark. Checkpoint — the shells spring open and the kitchen smells of garlic steam and ocean. Off the heat the moment the majority gape; a mussel that refused its colleagues gets one more minute in the residual steam and then, still shut, goes to the discard bowl — the unopened mussel is the one absolute no in shellfish.
  2. Gochujang and sesame oil in, swirled through the liquor off the heat until the pot runs glossy orange-gold; scallion greens over. Checkpoint — the broth test with the eating spoon: briny, buttery, garlic-sweet, a warm gochujang hum behind — it should taste like the sea made a sauce. Flat? A squeeze of lemon and a pinch of salt; this pot is forgiving.
  3. Pan to the table on its trivet, shells bowl beside, bread or rice armed. The eating: pull the mussels from the shells by hand or with an empty shell as tongs (the tradition's own tool), a mussel and a spoon of broth per bite, bread for the pot's floor. Checkpoint — the empty-pot test: no broth left, the bread has been torn to ragged edges, someone has asked what was in the sauce. That is the record finished properly.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Live mussels keep 1–2 days on ice in the fridge in an open bowl (never sealed — they breathe). Cooked, 1 day, meat picked for pasta or rice; the strained broth is the next day's soup base. - Variations: the oyster sauce (#SF12's butter-gochujang build) transplants whole; clams run the same pot at a shorter clock (#SF15); a splash of soju in the steam for the tent's version.
