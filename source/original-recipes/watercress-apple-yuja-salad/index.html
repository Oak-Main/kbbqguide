Watercress-Apple Yuja Salad

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes, serve within the hour · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The greens and fruit

  • 150 g watercress, thick stems trimmed, washed and spun dry
  • 1 crisp sweet apple (about 200 g), cut into matchsticks (skin on)

The dressing

  • 1 tbsp yuja-cheong (citron marmalade, about 25 g) — or honey plus half a lemon's zest and juice
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp soy sauce (5 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • Black pepper; a pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g)

Instructions

  1. The dressing whisked — yuja, vinegar, soy, sesame oil, gochugaru — into a light amber emulsion. Checkpoint — tastes bright-citrus first, then sesame; distinctly lighter than the slaw's dressing (the watercress brings its own pepper, so the dressing steps back).
  2. Watercress and apple folded through with a lifting turn, sesame and pepper over. Checkpoint — the eating test: the pepper of the cress, the apple's cold sweet snap, the yuja's citrus-aromatics over both, and the sesame oil's warmth binding — the bowl should taste like winter's argument for eating greens anyway. Beside jangjorim's (#B20) salt-depth, it is exactly the bright counter the jar wants.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Eats within the hour; the dressing keeps a week in a jar and improves. - Variations: Asian pear for apple (#F06's bridge); blanched minari for watercress; the dressing over winter citrus segments alone as a dessert-adjacent bowl.
