**Stream: restaurant — the macaroni bowl of the grill halls.** Every Korean barbecue restaurant that opened across America in the last forty years put a cold macaroni salad on the banchan tray, and every table ate it first: small pasta in a sweet mayo dressing with cucumber and carrot, cold and crunchy and mild — the cooling valve between rounds of galbi (#M03) and bulgogi (#M01). It has no deep lineage and needs none; it is a restaurant invention that earned its seat. This record is the style as the grill halls run it.

- **Boil the pasta one minute past the box's al dente.** This is the anti-Italian rule of the genre: the cold salad firms as it chills, and pasta cooked properly hot turns stiff-cold; a minute extra holds the tender chew after the chill. Rinse under cold water to stop the cooking and cool it — the one pasta record where rinsing is correct. - Salt and squeeze the cucumber (#B07's law — it is the water law of every cold bowl on this site); boil the egg with the pasta's first minutes.

Korean BBQ Restaurant-Style Macaroni Salad

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 10 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes · Serves 6–8 as banchan

Ingredients

The pasta

  • 250 g small macaroni or small shells (about 2.5 cups dry)

The dressing

  • 4 tbsp Kewpie-style mayonnaise (about 60 g)
  • 1 tbsp condensed milk or sugar (about 20 g) — the grill-hall sweetness signature
  • 1 tsp rice vinegar (5 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)

The mix-ins

  • 1/2 cucumber (about 100 g), small-diced, salted 10 minutes and squeezed
  • 1 small carrot (about 60 g), fine-diced or grated
  • 1 hard-boiled egg (about 50 g), chopped
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), sliced, optional

Instructions

  1. The dressing whisked smooth first — mayo, condensed milk, vinegar, salt — tasting distinctly sweeter than seems right at room temperature. It is: cold suppresses sweetness, and the chill will bring it to target. Checkpoint — the dressing tastes sweet-tangy, pourable, just shy of a sauce — it will be swallowed by the pasta, so run it loose.
  2. The drained, cooled, still-damp pasta and the dressing tossed while the pasta is cool but not fridge-cold (the dressing coats evenly at this temperature; fridge-cold pasta repels mayo), then the cucumber, carrot, egg, and scallion folded through. Checkpoint — every noodle glossed, the mix-ins distributed, the bowl holding together without a puddle at the bottom. If it pools, the cucumber's squeeze was skipped.
  3. Chill 30 minutes minimum, taste, and adjust — cold macaroni salad always needs one more grain of salt and a few drops of vinegar at the finish. Checkpoint — the eating test between bites of bulgogi (#M01): cold, sweet, soft-with-crunch, mild — the palate's reset button, which is the entire reason the grill halls serve it and the reason this bowl rides alongside the richest plates.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2 days refrigerated; refresh at serving with a spoon of mayo and a drop of vinegar — the bowl revives well. - Variations: the potato sibling (#B21); finely diced ham for the lunchbox build; a pinch of gochugaru over the top for the table that wants even this bowl to carry a little heat.
