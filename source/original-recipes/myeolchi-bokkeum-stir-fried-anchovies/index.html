**Stream: heritage — the drinking table's crunch.** Dried baby anchovies, toasted in a pan with garlic, soy, and sweetness until they turn from stiff to crisp-chewy, glazed and sesame-dusted: myeolchi bokkeum is the banchan that doubles as the anju — the snack that appears beside beer and soju at every drinking table in the country. Myeolchi Bokkeum Stir-Fried Anchovies

Prep: 5 minutes · Cook: 10 minutes · Total: About 15 minutes · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Ingredients

The anchovies and nuts

  • 150 g dried baby anchovies (myeolchi, the tiny 2–3 cm grade — not the large ones, which want a different dish)
  • 2 tbsp crushed roasted peanuts or walnuts (about 20 g), optional but the classic drinking build

The glaze

  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (about 12 g)
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp corn syrup or honey (about 30 g)
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g), optional
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

Instructions

  1. **The dry toast first — the technique.** Dry pan, medium heat, anchovies tossed for 4–5 minutes until they smell toasted-nutty and turn matte and snap-crisp (break one: it should crack, not bend). Checkpoint — the smell shift is unmistakable: from the fish-market jar smell to warm, toasty, almost nut-like. This pre-toast is the difference between crisp-chewy perfection and the tough, faintly damp disappointing version.
  2. Push the anchovies to the pan's side, oil into the empty half, garlic in until it sizzles blond (30 seconds), then the soy, syrup, and gochugaru stirred into the oil — a bubbling glaze — anchovies and nuts tossed through, 1–2 minutes of constant motion as the glaze tightens and coats. Checkpoint — every anchovy wears gloss, the pan is nearly dry, and a test piece has the target texture: crisp shell, faint chew at the core, sweet-salty-heat. The 60-second window between glazed and tough is the only clock in the recipe — off the heat when they still seem slightly soft, because they crisp further as they cool.
  3. Off the heat: sesame oil and seeds, a final toss, spread on a plate to cool in a single layer (piled hot, they steam each other soft). Checkpoint — fully cooled, an anchovy snaps cleanly. The bowl is ready — and it keeps, in a sealed jar, for two to three weeks, which makes it the banchan board's only long-shelf member.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The keeper: 2–3 weeks in a sealed jar at room temperature — the make-ahead banchan for a whole month of grill nights. - Variations: peanuts are the classic partner; a heavier gochugaru hand for the soju table; the same technique on dried shrimp (the smaller cousin, saeu-bokkeum) runs sweeter and faster.
