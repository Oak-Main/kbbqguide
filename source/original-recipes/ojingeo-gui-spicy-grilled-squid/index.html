Ojingeo Gui Spicy Grilled Squid

Korean · Medium · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the street-and-tavern grill.** Ojingeo gui is whole grilled squid, Korea's pojangmacha (street tent) and drinking-table classic: one squid, scored, rushed over charcoal, brushed with a sweet-spicy gochujang glaze in the last minutes, cut into rings at the table, and eaten with the dipping sauce and cold beer or soju that the dish was engineered to sell. It looks like restaurant theater and cooks like a technique lesson — squid has exactly one doneness question (2 minutes or 2 hours, nothing between), and this record is a masterclass in the fast answer.

The table

- **Score the bodies — inside faces.** Lay each body open flat (it is a tube; cut along one side and open it like a book). Score the inner face in a diagonal crosshatch, 5 mm apart, shallow cuts only — the blade should never go through. Two things happen on the fire: the scored faces curl and open into blossoms, and the increased surface area catches glaze and char. Leave the outer face smooth. - **Dry the squid thoroughly** with paper towel. Squid carries water and that water is the enemy of char; a wet squid boils gray in its own steam before it can toast. - **Make the glaze and the dip before the fire.** Whisk the glaze smooth and set it at the grill station with a brush; stir the dip together and park it on the table — the squid's speed at the end leaves no time for assembly. - **Fire hot, with distance control:** charcoal burned to gray-edge with the grate close (a palm's count of 2 seconds above the coals, roughly 250 °C at the surface), and the option to raise it.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The squid: cleaned, skinned, inner-face crosshatch at 5 mm, bone-dry before the fire.
  • The grill: hot and close (about 250 °C at the surface), dry sear first, glaze after the flip only.
  • The pull: scored face toasted, glaze glossy, body springy-with-give before the four-minute mark.
  • The table: scissors to rings, dip ready before the fire, cold drinks standing by — the dish's whole grammar.

Finish & Plate

The whole cut squid goes to the table's center on a warm board, rings and tentacles mixed, the dip bowl beside, radish batons and rice in reach. The last tentacle goes to whoever lost count of the soja glasses; the street-tent rule is the oldest rule.

Ojingeo Gui Spicy Grilled Squid

Prep: 25 minutes, plus 30 minutes marinating · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 1 hour 5 minutes · Serves 4

Ingredients

The squid

  • 4 whole squid (about 700 g cleaned weight), bodies 15–20 cm, cleaned, skins peeled, tentacles separated

The scoring and the salt

  • 1 tsp coarse salt (about 5 g) and a light grind of pepper

The glaze

  • 2 tbsp gochujang (about 40 g)
  • 1.5 tbsp honey or corn syrup (about 30 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g)

The dip (mayak style)

  • 3 tbsp soy sauce (45 ml), 1 tbsp rice vinegar (15 ml), 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g), 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g), 1 scallion (about 15 g) sliced, 1/2 Korean chili or jalapeño (about 8 g) sliced into rings

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 500 g cooked), soju or beer's ceremony, mu sangchae (thin radish batons, about 100 g) to cut the heat

Instructions

  1. **Dry-grill first, glaze second.** The scored bodies go on scored-face-down over the hot fire, tentacles beside them. Ninety seconds undisturbed — the bodies will curl and the scores will gape open, toasting in their grooves; the tentacle tips will char at their edges. Checkpoint — the scored face shows toasted brown in the grooves with light char at the curling edges; the smell is sweet-iodine ocean toast (this is correct — squid's smell on fire should be oceanic, never sulfurous; a sulfurous fish-market smell means the squid was past its day). If the bodies stick, the grate was not hot enough — a hot grate releases squid the way it releases skin.
  2. **Flip, glaze, finish.** Turn the bodies and tentacles, brush the toasted faces with glaze, 45–60 seconds more, and a second thin brush-stroke over each. The glaze needs only to set glossy — it is not cooking, it is dressing. Checkpoint — the glaze foams lightly and turns brick-glossy. Pull the bodies when they are just firm to the touch — springy with give, not tight as a drum. Tight-as-drum is the rubber door, and it closes at about the four-minute mark for bodies this size.
  3. **Cut at the table.** The scissors are the service: bodies crosswise into wide rings (the scored blossoms open as you cut), tentacles in halves. Straight from the board into the dip, or over rice with the glaze's drippings. Checkpoint — eating test, a ring straight from the dip: snap-tender bite (the 2-minute squid's signature), char in the score grooves, the glaze's sweet-heat, the dip's vinegar and chili behind it. Any rubbery chew means some piece crossed the doneness line — pull earlier next batch; squid cannot be saved, only timed.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Raw squid keeps 1 day on ice; glaze and dip keep a week (the dip improves). Cooked rings keep 1 day and make a fierce stir-fried rice or noodle addition, reheated hard and fast — remember, this record is the 2-minute pole of squid's doneness law; the 2-hour pole is a different dish entirely (braised ojingeo-jjim, not this record). - Variations: the clean-lane fork skips the glaze for soy-garlic baste and the #SF02 register; baby octopus runs this same glaze on the jjukkumi record (#SF06); the dip doubles as the sauce for cold boiled eggs, a pojangmacha two-fer.
