Gamja Jorim Soy-Braised Potatoes

Korean · Easy · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the caramelized braise.** Gamja jorim is the banchan that converts potato people on first bite: small chunks braised in soy, sugar, and garlic until the edges caramelize and glaze and the interiors drink the braise. It sits beside the grill's meats doing carbohydrate duty with a fork-sweetness that rice never attempts — the children's champion and the adults' quiet favorite of the banchan board. The whole technique is a reduced braise with corn syrup's gloss, finished fast.

Gamja Jorim Soy-Braised Potatoes

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 20 minutes · Total: About 30 minutes · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Ingredients

The potatoes

  • 700 g small yellow or red potatoes, cut into 3 cm chunks (skin on — the skin holds the chunk together through the braise)

The braise

  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (about 12 g)
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), sliced
  • 80 ml water
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp corn syrup or honey (about 30 g)
  • 1 tsp sugar (about 4 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml), off heat
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g); 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced; a pinch of gochugaru or sliced chili (about 1 g), optional

Instructions

  1. Oil and garlic in a wide pan over medium until the garlic just turns blond (30 seconds — blond, not brown; browned garlic runs bitter through a sweet braise), potatoes in, stirred to coat, 2 minutes of sizzling together. Checkpoint — the potato edges turn translucent-gold and the pan smells of toasted garlic — the braise's flavor floor just got laid.
  2. Water, soy, corn syrup, and sugar in; stir, lid on, medium-low. Ten minutes at a gentle bubble, stirring once at the halfway mark. Checkpoint — a paring knife into the largest chunk slides in with no resistance — the potato is cooked, and everything from here is reduction, not cooking. If the knife catches, three more minutes, lid on.
  3. **Lid off, and the reduction — the technique.** High-medium now, stirring and turning every 60–90 seconds as the liquid boils down. The braise goes from soupy to syrupy to glaze: the pan's liquids thicken, the sugars caramelize on the turned potato faces, and the potatoes bounce in a slow thick sizzle instead of a boil. Five to six minutes, off the heat the moment the glaze coats and the pan is nearly dry. Checkpoint — a test chunk: glazed dark at two or three faces, glossy, and it holds its shape but yields to the tooth — the braise has painted it, not dissolved it. Burnt-sugar smell is the one-second warning: off the heat now, not next stir. Sesame oil, seeds, scallion over, off the heat.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 3 days refrigerated and reheats beautifully (pan, medium, 3 minutes, a splash of water to loosen the glaze) — the make-ahead banchan par excellence, arguably better on day two. - Variations: the same braise on lotus root (#B18) and on quail eggs (#B19); a chili half in the braise for the drinking table; day-after glazed potatoes beside a fried egg is the lunchbox classic.
