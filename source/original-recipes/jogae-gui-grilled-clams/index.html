Jogae Gui Grilled Clams

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the grill-bar basics, perfected.** Jogae gui is the plainest shellfish record and the one every grill bar runs all summer: a mesh of clams on the grate, a bowl of dipping sauce, and the shells opening on their own schedule — each one pulled the moment it gapes and eaten hot with the dip. No butter, no build: the clam's own liquor and the salt-soy dip are the entire dish. The technique is the purge before and the eye at the fire after.

Jogae Gui Grilled Clams

Prep: 15 minutes (plus 30 minutes purging) · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 55 minutes, mostly purging · Serves 4

Ingredients

The clams

  • 1.2 kg small hard-shell clams (littleneck, manila, or vongole), mixed sizes welcome

The purge

  • Cold salted water (about 3 tbsp salt per liter, close to seawater), 30 minutes

The dip (gireumjang's clam cousin — keep it sharp and simple)

  • 3 tbsp soy sauce (45 ml)
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar (15 ml)
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), finely sliced
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g), 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml), a pinch of garlic (about 2 g), grated

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 400 g cooked) or noodles later — see Note — and the shell bowl

Instructions

  1. Clams onto the moderate grate in a single layer, hollow-side up where the shells allow. Nothing happens for 2–3 minutes — then they begin to pop open one by one, unevenly, each on its own clock. Checkpoint — an opened clam shows the meat just firmed at the edges and its liquor steaming — that one comes off now, not when its neighbors open. The grill boss works by eye, tongs ready, pulling gapers continuously.
  2. **The discard law at the fire:** a clam that stays fully shut after its batch-mates have opened (a full 2-minute grace) does not get forced or knifed — it goes to the discard bowl. Same law as the mussels (#SF13) and oysters (#SF12): the unopened shell is the sea's own warning label. Checkpoint — a test clam with the dip: snap-tender meat, briny liquor, the dip's soy-vinegar line behind it. If the meat is mushy (not springy), that clam was past its day — one is possible in any batch, a pattern means the batch was.
  3. The pace is the meal: pull, dip, eat, repeat — the platter refills from the grate in waves of five or six, and the last clams off (the stubborn ones) are often the sweetest. Checkpoint — the finish: shells in the bowl, dip nearly gone, and the clam liquor that dripped on the grate has caramelized into a smell the whole table notices — the traditional end at the grill bar is a handful of noodles or rice onto that spot to catch it. Do it; it is the record's secret last course.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Live clams keep 2 days in an open bowl in the fridge with a damp cloth over them. Cooked, 1 day, meats picked for pasta or jjigae. The dip keeps 3 days. - Variations: the butter-gochujang build from the oyster record (#SF12) on the larger clam shells; the mussel pot (#SF13) is the same clams' steamed cousin; the grill's caramelize-and-noodle finish (Method step 3) is the tradition's own variation on itself.
