Korean-Style Gochujang Grilled Pork Ribs

Korean · Medium · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: contemporary.** This is the grill-country bridge record: country-style pork ribs — those thick, meaty shoulder chops sold as ribs — wearing a gochujang marinade built for low-and-slow-ish grilling rather than the tabletop's speed rounds. It is not a traditional recipe with a lineage to defend; it is the yangnyeom lane's flavor set applied to a cut that loves it, written honestly as a modern build. The technique worth keeping is the two-stage fire: gentle roasting first to render and cook through, then a hot finish to set the glaze.

Korean-Style Gochujang Grilled Pork Ribs

Prep: 20 minutes, plus 8–24 hours marinating · Cook: 40 minutes · Total: About 1 hour 10 minutes (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The ribs

  • 1.5 kg country-style pork ribs (boneless shoulder-cut, about 6 pieces), or bone-in pork ribs cut into individual ribs

The marinade-glaze

  • 4 tbsp gochujang (about 80 g)
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce (45 ml)
  • 2 tbsp honey (about 40 g)
  • 2 tbsp mirin (30 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 6 cloves garlic (about 24 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 15 g), grated
  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 150 g), grated and squeezed nearly dry
  • 2 tsp gochugaru (about 6 g), optional
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), kimchi (#B01), musaengchae (#B08), lettuce ssam

Instructions

  1. **Stage one — roast gentle.** Thick-side down on the cool zone, lid down, turning every 8–10 minutes, 25–30 minutes total. The fat renders slowly and the interior cooks through without the sugars involved yet. No glaze in this stage — glaze on raw heat is the burned-sugar trap. Checkpoint — at stage one's end the meat is firm-poached looking, opaque to the center at a test cut edge (pork's rule: no pink for the cautious table, a faint blush acceptable at exactly 70 °C internal if you measure), and the surface is dry-ish and ready to take glaze.
  2. **Stage two — glaze hot.** Move the ribs to the hot zone. Brush the reserved marinade on both faces, turning every 90 seconds, building two or three thin coats — 6–8 minutes total. Each thin coat sets glossy; one thick coat burns. Checkpoint — the surface should go brick-red and tacky-glossy, charred only at the fat edges. If any face blackens, it has had enough fire — back to the cool zone to rest.
  3. Rest 5 minutes, cut between the bones (or into thick slices for boneless), sesame over, platter. Checkpoint — eating test: the glaze's sweet-heat edge, then rendered-soft shoulder meat that pulls tender under the tooth — this should eat like the best version of backyard ribs that a Korean pantry makes, and the honest note at the table is that it is a modern hybrid, not an old recipe wearing a new one.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated ribs keep 24 hours, freeze 1 month. Cooked, 4 days — chop into fried rice or warm into a rice bowl with the last of the reserved glaze. - Variations: the marinade doubles on chicken thighs (#M17); a spoon of honey in the final coat for a stickier finish; ssam service with perilla leaf is the Korean table's claim on the platter.
