Soy-Garlic Grilled Shrimp

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 20 minutes marinating · Cook: 6 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes · Serves 3–4

Ingredients

The shrimp

  • 700 g large shrimp (16–20 count), shell on, deveined

The marinade

  • 1.5 tbsp soy sauce (about 22 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), grated
  • 1 tsp honey (about 4 g) — structure, not sweetness
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)

The dip and table

  • Yangnyeom ganjang (#SA04), the scallion-soy dip, in a bowl the table shares
  • Steamed rice (about 400 g cooked), kimchi (#B01), lettuce ssam

Instructions

  1. Hot grate or pan, single layer, two minutes undisturbed. The shells take light char at the tips; the smell is garlic and sesame over toasting shell. Checkpoint — the shells blush and the bottom edges char thinly; lift one — the meat below should be opaque more than halfway up.
  2. Flip once, 60–90 seconds, off the fire the moment the last translucence at the center of a test shrimp is gone. Checkpoint — pull one and cut: opaque white through, still plump and springy. Shrimp's overcook line is 30 seconds past this point — the grill boss eats the test piece standing up, as is right.
  3. Platter, and the dip is half the dish: yangnyeom ganjang (#SA04), scallions heavy, each shrimp dunked at the table. Checkpoint — eating test: snap, sweetness, soy-garlic roundness, the dip's scallion bite — shrimp first, seasoning behind, always. This lane exists to let the shrimp talk.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Raw marinated shrimp hold 30 minutes. Cooked, 2 days — cold peeled shrimp over rice or into a light noodle bowl; shells to stock. - Variations: a pinch of gochugaru in the marinade to walk toward the spicy fork (#SF01); butter in the final minute for the fusion table; the same marinade on the squid record's clean cousin lanes.
