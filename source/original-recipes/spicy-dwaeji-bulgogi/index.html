Spicy Dwaeji Bulgogi

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the gochujang pork lane.** Dwaeji bulgogi is pork's answer to the bulgogi (#M01) idea, dressed in the yangnyeom lane's red paste: thin pork slices in a gochujang-gochugaru marinade, grilled fast and hot, folded into ssam or heaped over rice. Where beef bulgogi runs sweet and juicy, its pork cousin runs hot and savory-sweet — the fat of a shoulder slice carries the paste's ferment better than any lean meat could. This is the spice-first workhorse of the Korean grill table, and the record every weeknight cook should own from this site. Name it honestly at your table: it shares architecture with bulgogi, but it is the yangnyeom lane's dish, not soy-slurry bulgogi with a pepper swap. Who cooks what: one grill boss on the batches, the table on ssam — the pork cooks so fast that the assembly line matters more than the fire.

- **Blend the paste smooth.** Gochujang whisked with the soy, honey, and mirin first; then the gochugaru, sesame oil, garlic, ginger, onion, and the squeezed pear. The pear is enzyme and a whisper of fruit, not the sweet engine it is in #M01 — squeeze it dry or the marinade steams instead of chars. - **Marinate 2–8 hours, refrigerated.** Two is honest for a weeknight; the pork's fat protects it where a lean beef would mush, so the window is generous. - **Plan the heat before you mix, not after.** The gochugaru is the dial: 1 tablespoon for a table that says "a little spicy," 2 for the honest default, more for nobody. Gochujang heat builds through the meal — set the dial for bite fifteen, not bite one. - Pull the pork out 20 minutes before the fire; cold slices on a hot grate steam gray at the edges.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The slice: 4 mm against the grain, shoulder or collar, marinated 2–8 hours in a smooth paste.
  • The grill: single loose layer, flip at the beading edge, brick-brown not black, under three minutes a batch.
  • The pull: juicy centers with a blush of juice, never gray-through.
  • The table: ssam or rice-bowl, cold banchan in reach, heat dialed for the end of the meal.

Finish & Plate

Platter at the center, sesame and scallion over the glistening red slices, and the pan's juice scraped into a small bowl for the rice. Ssam leaves, ssamjang (#SA01), garlic coins, and the cold cucumber (#B07) within every hand's reach — this record's table runs hot, and the cold crunch is not optional garnish.

Spicy Dwaeji Bulgogi

Prep: 20 minutes, plus 2–8 hours marinating · Cook: 12 minutes at the table · Total: About 35 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The pork

  • 900 g pork shoulder or collar (moksal), sliced 4 mm against the grain (ask for dwaeji bulgogi slice)

The marinade

  • 3 tbsp gochujang (about 60 g)
  • 2 tbsp gochugaru (about 12 g) — the ceiling dial; 1 tbsp for a mild table
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce (45 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp honey or brown sugar (about 30 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 6 cloves garlic (about 24 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 15 g), grated
  • 1/2 onion (about 75 g), grated
  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 150 g), grated and squeezed nearly dry
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), sliced; 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), kimchi (#B01), thick garlic coins, and cold oi muchim (#B07) — heat on the plate wants cold crunch beside it

Instructions

  1. **Fire hot, batches small.** Charcoal or cast-iron at a solid medium-high — a pan surface around 200 °C, oil just at the shimmer. The slices go on in one loose layer — piled pork steams in the paste's moisture, and steamed paste is bitter. Single layer, sixty to ninety seconds undisturbed. Checkpoint — the paste should sizzle and darken one shade, and the air should carry toasted chile and sesame. Black edges in the first minute mean the fire is a notch past the paste's tolerance — raise the grate or bank the coals and continue.
  2. **Flip at the weep, pull fast.** Turn the slices when the edges bead juice and the underside is brick-brown, 60–90 seconds more on the second side, off the fire while the centers still carry a blush of juice. Pork shoulder at 4 mm is done in under three minutes a batch; every extra ten seconds trades juice for toughness. Checkpoint — test piece at the grill station: glazed edge, juicy-fatty interior, heat arriving after the sweet on the finish. If the center is dry, the batch was thin-spread and overcooked — next batch spends less time on the second side.
  3. **The two ways to eat it, both correct.** The ssam way: leaf, thin ssamjang swipe, a hot slice, garlic coin, fold, one bite — the record's full ceremony. The weeknight way: rice in a bowl, a stack of slices over, sesame and scallion, the pan's juice spooned last. Same meat, two speeds. Checkpoint — the bowl test: the juice over rice should be red-gold and savory-sweet, milder than the slices taste plain — if it runs bitter, the pan's sugared drippings caught fire at some point and the heat was a notch high throughout; note it for next time, eat around it now.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated pork keeps 24 hours (freeze flat up to a month). Cooked, 3 days — and the leftovers are arguably the record's second life: kimchi fried rice with diced dwaeji bulgogi, or a melt-grade cheese-and-dwaeji quesadilla that the fusion lane would claim if it could. - Variations: the same paste on pork belly slabs (#M11's slower cousin); on chicken thighs (#M17); thinned with mirin into a glaze for the shrimp record (#SF01). The soy-pork fork without heat is ganjang pork bulgogi (#M14).
