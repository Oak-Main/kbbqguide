**Stream: restaurant — the steakhouse bowl.** The onion-soy dip is the tabletop steakhouse's bowl: sweet onion macerated in soy and vinegar until it turns translucent and half-pickled, served ice-cold beside the big beef cuts. Where the scallion build (#SA04) is sharp and green, this one is sweet-rounded — onion's sugars soften the soy's salt and the vinegar turns it all bright — and its cold temperature against a just-off-the-grill rib is the entire reason it exists. It is also the easiest make-ahead on the site: ten minutes, a night in the fridge, better for it.

Korean BBQ Onion Soy-Vinegar Dipping Sauce

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 10 minutes · Makes about 200 g

Ingredients

The sauce

  • 1 small sweet onion (about 150 g), halved and sliced paper-thin (thinness is the recipe — see Method)
  • 5 tbsp soy sauce (75 ml)
  • 3 tbsp rice vinegar (45 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp honey or sugar (about 30 g)
  • 1 tbsp water (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • A pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional color and warmth
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced, optional

Instructions

  1. Soy, vinegar, honey, and water stirred until the honey dissolves completely — the base should taste like a bright, slightly sweet soy dressing, sharper than it will finish (the onion's sugar will round it). Checkpoint — a fingertip taste: salt, then a clean sour snap, then sweetness landing third. When in doubt, sharp — the onion sweetens everything.
  2. The onion slices loosened into rings, into the liquid, pressed under with a stir; sesame oil, seeds, gochugaru over. Refrigerate 30 minutes to overnight. Checkpoint — at serving, the onion rings should be translucent-soft with just a spine of crunch, the liquid turned faintly pink from the gochugaru, and the whole bowl cold enough to fog the ceramic. The taste: soy rounder than it started, vinegar brighter through the onion's sugar — a sauce that tastes like it cooked for hours and touched no heat.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2 days refrigerated and peaks on day one overnight. The onion is the perishable; the liquid alone keeps a week and restarts with a fresh onion. - Variations: a few drops of lemon for a sharper cold build; diced pear alongside the onion for the fruit bowl; the same liquid over thin-sliced cucumber for a five-minute banchan — the sauce is really a quick-pickle brine with a table career.
