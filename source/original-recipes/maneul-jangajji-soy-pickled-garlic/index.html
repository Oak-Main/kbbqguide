Maneul Jangajji Soy-Pickled Garlic

Korean · Easy · Makes 1 jar (a month's table)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the patience jar.** Whole garlic cloves, peeled and sunk into a soy-vinegar brine, wait a week and turn into something the raw clove never promised: dark, mellow, sweet-sour-savory, the fierce allium heat dissolved into a condiment-cloak that the grill table eats whole beside rich meat. Maneul jangajji is the garlic-lover's jar and the discipline lesson in one: twenty minutes of work, and the recipe is genuinely not ready until the week is up.

The brine

- **Peel whole, and trim nothing else.** The clove's root-end and its flat base stay intact — cut garlic in brine goes murky and sharp; whole cloves keep the brine clear and the texture snappy-firm. The wide-jar smash-peel method (smash the whole head lightly, the skins lift off) saves ten minutes. - Sterilize the jar: hot soapy wash, a rinse in boiling water, air-dry. A 500 ml jar takes this batch exactly.

Maneul Jangajji Soy-Pickled Garlic

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 5 minutes · Total: 20 minutes, ready in 1 week · Makes 1 jar (a month's table)

Ingredients

The garlic

  • 300 g fresh garlic (about 3 heads), peeled to whole cloves — the fresh-juicy season's garlic, not the sprouting-end-of-winter kind

The brine

  • 150 ml soy sauce
  • 150 ml water
  • 60 ml rice vinegar
  • 3 tbsp sugar or honey (about 40 g)
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns (about 3 g), optional
  • 1 dried red chili (about 1 g), optional
  • A splash of toasted sesame oil and a pinch of sesame seeds (about 3 ml / 2 g), at serving

Instructions

  1. The brine's ingredients to a boil and 2 minutes' simmer — this dissolves the sugar fully and pasteurizes the liquid (the pickling's one safety formality), then off the heat to cool 5 minutes, still hot-warm. Checkpoint — the brine tastes aggressively salty-sweet-sour at a fingertip — correct. It is a concentrate; the garlic's water will round it.
  2. The cloves packed into the jar, peppercorns and chili tucked between, the warm brine poured over to cover fully, lid on loose while it cools, then sealed and refrigerated once room temperature. Checkpoint — every clove submerged (a small folded parchment or a weight helps the floaters under), the brine already darkening the clove skins' edges. The jar's clock starts now.
  3. **The week.** Day 1 to day 7, the garlic transforms — green-white to amber to deep mahogany-tan, the raw heat leaving, the sweetness arriving. Taste a clove at day 5 (it will be sharp-edged still) and again at day 7. Checkpoint — the day-7 test, the readiness definition: a clove bitten whole tastes mellow-sweet-savory with a soft, faintly spicy finish — completely changed, and exactly the banchan. Earlier is sharper; two weeks is deeper. The jar rides for a month.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCauseFix
Brine cloudy, murkCut garlic or skipped the simmerWhole cloves; the brine always simmered

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The jar IS the recipe's storage: a month refrigerated, improving for two. The brine's second career (seasoning) closes the loop. - Variations: the honey-forward fork (swap the sugar for honey — the café style); soy-only, no vinegar, for the deep-savory jar; the perilla-leaf sibling (#B24) shares the shelf and the patience.
