**Stream: restaurant — the seaside grill bar.** Grilled oysters on the half shell, a gochujang-scallion butter melting in each cup, is the seaside grill bar's version of the raw-bar classic — the shell becomes the vessel, the fire does the opening, and the sauce is the Korean pantry's answer to garlic butter. This is the easiest showpiece on the seafood list: shuck or no-shuck, ten minutes from grill to platter, and the table eats them like soup.

Scallion-Gochujang Grilled Oysters

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes · Serves 4 (makes 12)

Ingredients

The oysters

  • 12 fresh oysters in the shell, scrubbed (medium size — large cups hold more sauce)

The sauce

  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (about 30 g), melted
  • 1 tbsp gochujang (about 20 g)
  • 1 tsp honey (about 4 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 4 scallions (about 60 g), finely sliced
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)

The table

  • A folded towel or rock salt to steady the shells, lemon wedges, rice or just the platter and drinks

Instructions

  1. **Whole (no-shuck):** cup-down on the moderate grate, lid or foil tent over, 5–8 minutes. They will hiss, gap, and sometimes snap audibly — each one that opens comes off immediately, knife-tip through the hinge, top shell off. Checkpoint — an opened oyster shows the liquor steaming and the meat just firmed at the ruffle's edge — poached, not dried. An oyster that stays shut after 8 minutes gets one more minute and then, still shut, goes to the discard pile — the raw-bar safety rule transplanted to the grill: a cooker that will not open is telling you something.
  2. **Sauce each cup:** a spoon of the gochujang butter into the hot liquor — it foams and melds in the shell — scallion over, back on the grill (or under a broiler) for 60–90 seconds to set. Checkpoint — the sauce bubbles at the shell's edge and smells of butter, chile, and ocean. The meat should still be plump and just-set; over-sauced cups that boiled hard taste of butter alone.
  3. **Shucked-ahead path, for the confident:** top shell off, a spoon of sauce in each cup, cup-down on the moderate grill 4–5 minutes until the edges frill and the sauce bubbles — same checkpoint, one step fewer at the fire. Checkpoint — eating test, the whole ceremony in one motion: tip the shell's contents into the mouth — liquor first, then the meat with the sauce's sweet-heat riding it, then the scallion's bite. The shells stack empty; the count is the scoreboard.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Live oysters keep 3–5 days refrigerated cup-down, covered with a damp cloth — never airtight, never on ice that melts into them. The sauce keeps a week. Grilled oysters are eat-now. - Variations: the mussels-and-clams extensions (#SF13, #SF15); a drop of the cho-gochujang (#SA03) in each cup instead of the butter build; shredded mozzarella over the sauce, the bus-stop kiosk style, for the record's one fully-fusion fork.
