Sangchu Geotjeori Fresh Lettuce Salad

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the lettuce bowl beyond the wrap.** Sangchu geotjeori takes the same lettuce that wraps the ssam (#F01) and dresses half a head of it as a quick, spicy, unfermented salad — the fresh-kimchi treatment (#B04's logic) run on the leaf. It is the lightest red bowl on the table, five minutes of work, and the one that makes a simple rice-and-meat lunch feel complete.

Sangchu Geotjeori Fresh Lettuce Salad

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes, best within 2 hours · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The lettuce

  • 1/2 head green or red leaf lettuce (about 300 g), torn into bite pieces, washed and spun bone-dry

The dressing

  • 1.5 tbsp gochugaru (about 9 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp honey (about 5 g)
  • 1 clove garlic (about 4 g), grated
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • A splash of rice vinegar (about 3 ml), optional
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced

Instructions

  1. The dressing stirred together in the serving bowl — gochugaru, soy, honey, garlic — into a loose paste. Checkpoint — tastes a shade too strong on a fingertip; the lettuce's water will round it, same law as every fresh bowl in this family.
  2. Lettuce in, tossed with two spoons or hands — #B04's massage minute — until every leaf wears the red-flecked gloss. Sesame oil, seeds, scallion over. Checkpoint — the leaf test: crisp, cold, dressed-through — spicy-savory on the first bite, the leaf's clean sweetness after, no dressing pooling at the bowl's floor. Serve beside any of the M-list's records; it is the freshest possible counter to the gochujang lanes (#M04, #M13).

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Undressed lettuce keeps 2 days in paper towel; the dressed bowl does not keep — by design. - Variations: cucumber pieces folded in; the cabbage version (#B04) for a sturdier bowl; a drop of cho-gochujang (#SA03) thinned as the dressing for the sweet-heat fork.
