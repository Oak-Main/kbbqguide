Hotteok Brown-Sugar Seed Pancakes

Korean · Medium · Makes 8 pancakes (serves 4)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the street cart's sweet.** Hotteok is the winter street cart's calling card: a soft yeasted dough, flattened in the palm, filled with a pocket of brown sugar, cinnamon, and seeds, then pan-fried until the crust crisps and the filling melts into molten syrup that stays dangerously hot inside. It is the grill meal's traditional walk-away dessert — the cart at the market's mouth after the table clears — and the one that ends this site's record list because it ends the meal in real life. The craft is a working dough, a sealed pocket, and the low-slow fry that melts the sugar without burning the crust.

The fry

- **Mix the dough wet and let it rise covered, 1 hour.** Hotteok dough is stickier than bread dough — that wetness is the soft interior's promise. Flour, sweet rice flour, sugar, salt, and yeast whisked dry; the warm milk in, mixed to a shaggy paste, the oil worked in, then covered in the bowl until doubled, puffy, and bubbling at the surface. **Checkpoint —** the doubled dough jiggles when the bowl is tapped and smells of yeast and sweetness. Punch it down and it re-inflates slowly — a working dough, not an exhausted one. - **Mix the filling in a dry bowl and set it beside the stove** with a small bowl of cold water for your hands and a plate for the shaped dough: the assembly is a production line and the fry waits for no one. The brown sugar's clumps broken fully — a hard clump in the pocket melts to a lava pocket that stays molten five minutes longer than the rest.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The dough: wet, risen an hour to doubled, shaped with oiled hands, sealed purses.
  • The fry: medium-low (160 °C's lazy shimmer), pressed once to 1.5 cm, deep-gold crust, cushion-soft interior.
  • The melt: the filling fully liquid at the first tear, seeds suspended in the syrup.
  • The serve: the one-minute rest, then straight from the rack — hotteok has no second act.

Finish & Plate

Two pancakes per person on a small plate with a napkin — the street cart's service, unchanged. Coffee or the sikhye (#D05) beside, and the table eats them leaning forward, which is the posture the dish has always demanded.

Hotteok Brown-Sugar Seed Pancakes

Prep: 20 minutes, plus 1 hour rising · Cook: 20 minutes · Total: About 1 hour 40 minutes · Makes 8 pancakes (serves 4)

Ingredients

The dough

  • 300 g all-purpose flour
  • 50 g sweet rice flour (chapssalgaru — the chew; substitute fine glutinous rice flour)
  • 30 g sugar
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)
  • 2 tsp instant yeast (about 7 g)
  • 250 ml warm milk or water (about 40 °C — warm to the wrist, not hot)
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (about 12 g), worked into the dough

The filling

  • 100 g dark brown sugar, packed
  • 2 tbsp sunflower or pine nuts, coarsely chopped (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp sesame seeds (about 8 g)
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon (about 3 g)
  • A pinch of salt (about 1 g)

The fry

  • Neutral oil for the pan (about 3 tbsp / 40 g total across the batch)

Instructions

  1. **Shape the pockets with oiled hands, not floured ones.** With wet or oiled palms, tear off a ball of dough (about 80 g), and flatten it in your hand into a 10 cm disc — the dough is too soft to roll; the palm is the rolling pin. A spoonful of filling (about 20 g) into the center, then the disc's edges gathered up and pinched closed like a drawstring purse, seam down on the plate. Eight of them. Checkpoint — each purse is fully sealed with no sugar showing (an open seam leaks molten sugar straight into the oil — the batch's one real hazard, both for the fry and for the sugar lost from the pancake) and the dough around the filling is even, without a thin blowout side.
  2. **Fry low and slow, flatten once.** A generously oiled pan at medium-low — the oil at a lazy shimmer, not a sizzle (around 160 °C if the pan could speak). Two or three purses in seam-side-down, 60–90 seconds to set the bottom, then flip and press each one flat with the spatula — one confident press into a 1.5 cm disc — and another 2–3 minutes on that side, flipping once more briefly to re-crisp the first face. Checkpoint — the crust goes deep golden and lacy-crisp at the pressed edges while the interior stays thick and pale-soft; press one at the center — it should give like a cushion. A dark crust at 90 seconds means the heat is running high and the filling inside is still grainy — the low-slow fry exists precisely so the sugar melts in the time the crust browns.
  3. **Rest one minute — the safety minute — and serve.** Off the pan to a rack or plate, and a full minute before the first bite: the filling is molten syrup at 120 °C-plus straight from the pan, the street cart's small legend of burned mouths and burned fingers. Checkpoint — the eating test: the first bite tears the crisp crust and the syrup runs — dark brown sugar, cinnamon, the seeds' crunch, the chewy-soft interior catching it all. It should be slightly too hot, slightly messy, and completely worth both. That is the street cart's exact register, reproduced.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Eats from the rack, full stop — see the Note. The dough keeps 24 hours refrigerated after the first rise (punch down, chill, warm 30 minutes before shaping); shaped purses 30 minutes under a damp towel. - Variations: the seed mix swaps freely; a spoon of cream cheese with the brown sugar for the modern carts' melted-cheese fork; the savory hotteok (scallion and soy) is a different cart's lunch — same dough, opposite filling, worth its own afternoon.
