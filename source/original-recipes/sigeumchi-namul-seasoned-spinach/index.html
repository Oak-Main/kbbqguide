Sigeumchi Namul Seasoned Spinach

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the three-minute namul.** Spinach namul is the fastest bowl in the whole banchan tradition: blanch, squeeze, dress. It is the gateway recipe that teaches the Korean vegetable logic — greens seasoned with sesame, garlic, and salt until they are a dish rather than a side of greens — and at the grill table it is the bowl that keeps appearing alongside the rich meats because it weighs nothing on the palate. Master this and the pattern (#B05's blanch-dress law) is yours forever.

The seasoning

- Wash the spinach hard — second rinse water should run clear. Spinach's crown holds the field's grit, and no seasoning masks sand. - Set the ice bath or a colander with cold running water ready; the shock after the blanch is what keeps the green green.

Sigeumchi Namul Seasoned Spinach

Prep: 5 minutes · Cook: 2 minutes · Total: About 12 minutes · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The spinach

  • 500 g spinach, mature bunched (not baby), roots trimmed, washed twice (grit hides in the crown)

The seasoning

  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 tsp soy sauce (5 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g), lightly crushed

Instructions

  1. Pot of unsalted water at a rolling boil, spinach in by the handful, stems first (hold the bunch tops-down, stems in the water twenty seconds, then plunge the leaves). Sixty to ninety seconds total — no more, the leaves cook in the time it takes to stir them under. Checkpoint — the leaves go vivid and just-wilted, stems bright and beginning to bend. Pull at vivid — the color at the pot is the color at the table, minus what overcooking steals.
  2. Straight into cold water to shock, then — the technique — squeeze by the fistful, hard. The spinach must leave the colander as compressed, nearly dry bundles; a wet spinach bowl is a diluted one. Roughly chop the squeezed bundles once or twice. Checkpoint — a squeezed bundle holds its shape in the hand and drips not at all. This squeezing is the entire difference between restaurant namul and the sad watery version.
  3. Season while the spinach is still faintly warm: sesame oil, salt, garlic, soy, sesame — tossed by hand, loosened to a bowl. Checkpoint — the taste: sesame-nut first, the green's mineral sweetness, garlic's hum, salt just behind. It should taste seasoned-through, not dressed-on-top — if the first bite is plain spinach and the second is garlic, the toss was short.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2 days refrigerated (the color holds because it was shocked); refresh at serving with a drop of sesame oil and a re-toss. - Variations: a pinch of gochugaru for color; blanched bean sprouts folded through (#B05's lane) for the two-green bowl; the same law on blanched chives, the pattern (#B16, #B17) continues.
