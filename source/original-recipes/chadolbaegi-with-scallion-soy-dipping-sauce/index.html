Chadolbaegi with Scallion-Soy Dipping Sauce

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant.** Chadolbaegi — paper-thin frozen beef brisket shavings — is the tabletop grill's speed round. The slices are cut while frozen hard, dropped on the grate still frost-cold, and cooked in seconds per side; the whole pleasure is the contrast of a crisp lacy edge against a melting, barely-set center, dunked in a sharp scallion-soy dip. It is the cheapest cut on the K-BBQ menu and routinely the favorite order, because chadolbaegi's ratio of drama to effort has no rival. Ten seconds of patience at the fire, and the fat does everything else.

- **Keep the meat frozen until the moment it grills.** This is the one record where "straight from the freezer" is the technique: thawed chadolbaegi welds to the grate and loses the shavings' shape. Chadolbaegi with Scallion-Soy Dipping Sauce

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 6 minutes · Total: About 20 minutes at the table · Serves 4

Ingredients

The beef

  • 700 g beef brisket, frozen hard and machine-sliced paper-thin (order "chadolbaegi" at a Korean market; freeze your own block 3–4 hours and have it sliced or shave it yourself on a mandoline)

The dip (yangnyeom ganjang, #SA04's quick build)

  • 4 tbsp soy sauce (60 ml)
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar (30 ml)
  • 4 scallions (about 60 g), finely sliced
  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (about 6 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp sugar (about 2 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), kimchi (#B01), sangchu geotjeori (#F03), soju's presence is traditional and optional

Instructions

  1. Lay the frozen shavings flat, one layer, as many as fit without touching. They cook in 8–12 seconds a side — the grill boss stands over them with tongs the entire time. The edge will crisp and frill while the center barely sets; that is the dish. Checkpoint — the color change runs pink-frost to gray-brown at the edges with the fat turning translucent. Pull the slice the moment it no longer looks raw; a fully cooked-through chadolbaegi slice is a ruined one.
  2. Dip while hot, straight from tongs to bowl to mouth. Rice between rounds; the fat is rich in a way that wants starch and acid beside it. Checkpoint — eating test: crisp edge, melting center, the dip's vinegar and scallion cutting the fattiness. If it tastes purely of fat, the dip is too gentle — more vinegar and gochugaru.
  3. Repeat in small batches. The shavings come off the freezer pack a handful at a time; any that thaw on the platter go on the grill immediately at a slightly cooler spot, or into the next round of fried rice instead. Checkpoint — by the last batch the dip bowl should be reduced and cloudy from repeated dunking — the traditional end state, and the sign of a full table.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The frozen block keeps 2 months sealed; thawed slices should be cooked the same day. The dip keeps 3 days refrigerated and improves overnight. - Variations: the dip builds toward cho-gochujang (#SA03) for a hotter table; leftover shavings into kimchi fried rice; the same dip on grilled mushrooms (#SF02's lane) carries the vegan table.
