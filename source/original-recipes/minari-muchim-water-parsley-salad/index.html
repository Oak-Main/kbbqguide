Minari Muchim Water Parsley Salad

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the spring water green.** Minari — water parsley, Korean watercress's sharper cousin — is the green that arrived with spring floods and got its own seat at the table: blanched for seconds, dressed warm in the namul set (#B06's family) or served as a cold, punchy salad beside rich grilled meat. Its flavor is celery, parsley, and pepper in one stem, and it is the most distinctive green on this site's fresh list. This record runs the blanched-dressed build, the more traditional of its two lives.

- Wash minari hard, twice — it grows in water and carries its field with it. Trim the fuzzy roots to the white crown; keep the stems whole, at their full 15 cm glory (the stem IS the dish). - Have the ice bath ready — the two-second blanch demands its cold landing.

Minari Muchim Water Parsley Salad

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 2 minutes · Total: About 20 minutes · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The minari

  • 300 g fresh minari (Korean water parsley) or watercress, roots trimmed, washed twice

The seasoning

  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 tsp soy sauce (5 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)
  • A pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional

The optional cold-salad fork (no blanch)

  • 1 tsp rice vinegar (5 ml) and 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g), swapped in for the warm seasoning

Instructions

  1. A pot of boiling water and the minari in for a count of ten — stems first, leaves under at the last three seconds — then straight to ice water. Checkpoint — the stems bright green and just-barely bent, the leaves vivid and untouched by cooking time. Ten seconds is not shorthand; minari's whole character is its snap, and thirty ruins it.
  2. Squeeze by the fistful — the #B06 law, the water out so the seasoning in — and cut the squeezed bundles once across, into 7 cm lengths. Checkpoint — dry, springy bundles that hold shape in the hand.
  3. Warm the seasoning in the bowl, minari in while the greens are still faintly warm, the full-minute hand toss (the massage is the recipe's only labor), sesame over. Checkpoint — the eating test: the stem's clean, peppery snap with sesame and garlic riding it — a green that tastes like it grew in moving water, which is exactly what it did. Beside samgyeopsal (#M10) it does the work a whole pickle plate attempts.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Blanched-dressed keeps 1 day; the raw fork eats same-day. Blanched, undressed bundles hold 2 days refrigerated. - Variations: the raw vinegar fork (see Note); mixed with spinach (#B06) for the two-green bowl; stems laid whole across the ssam platter (#F01) as the herbal thread in a wrap.
