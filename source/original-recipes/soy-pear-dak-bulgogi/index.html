Soy-Pear Dak Bulgogi

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

**Stream: restaurant.** Dak bulgogi is chicken's seat at the bulgogi table: thigh slices in the soy-pear marinade, grilled fast and hot like every other bulgogi lane record. The pear does more for chicken than for any other protein here — raw thigh is dense, and the enzyme work plus a real marinade window turns it tender and glossy. Thigh, not breast: this is a hard rule of the genre, and the record's only non-negotiable.

Soy-Pear Dak Bulgogi

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 2–6 hours marinating · Cook: 12 minutes at the table · Total: About 30 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The chicken

  • 900 g boneless, skinless chicken thighs, sliced 7 mm thick or left whole and butterflied

The marinade

  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 150 g), grated with juice
  • 1/2 onion (about 75 g), grated
  • 60 ml soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp honey or brown sugar (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 10 g), grated — ginger works harder here than in the beef lanes
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g); 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), kimchi (#B01), scallion threads

Instructions

  1. Medium-high fire, single layer, slices not touching. Chicken's surface water steams before it browns if the pan is crowded — space is crust. Checkpoint — the first side is undisturbed until the edges turn opaque and the underside is browned, about 2 minutes for slices, 4 for butterflied whole thighs.
  2. Flip once; the second side runs 1–2 minutes more for slices. Pull when the meat's juices run clear at a thick piece's cut edge and the surface is glossy brown — no pinkness anywhere, chicken's absolute rule. Checkpoint — test piece: glossy, tender, sweet-savory with ginger brightening the finish — chicken's mildness is the marinade's showcase, and every ingredient reads clearly here.
  3. Scissors into bite pieces, sesame and scallion over, warm platter. Ssam or rice bowl, both lanes open. Checkpoint — the rice bowl test: juice over rice first — the pan will have a glossy glaze of rendered fat and marinade that spoons like a sauce, milder and rounder than the beef version. That spoon is the record's quiet prize.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated chicken keeps 24 hours. Cooked, 3 days — cold slices over rice, or the weeknight bossam-style wrap with ssamjang. - Variations: the gochujang fork (#M17); the same marinade on wing segments glazed in the last minutes; pineapple swapped for pear in a pinch (shorter window — its enzyme is far stronger).
