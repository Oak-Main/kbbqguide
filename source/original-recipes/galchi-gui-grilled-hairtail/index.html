Galchi Gui Grilled Hairtail

Korean · Easy · Serves 3–4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the Jeju island classic.** Hairtail — galchi, the silver ribbon fish — is the fish Jeju islanders grill more than any other: a meter of solid meat and no bones but the spine, salted and grilled until the silver skin blisters gold. It is the mackerel record's (#SF07) leaner sibling: where mackerel runs fat and needs the fermented cut beside it, hairtail runs clean and sweet, and its record runs the same cure-and-grill law at a shorter clock. The skin, gone crisp, is the delicacy — protect it with the grate discipline you learned on the mackerel.

Galchi Gui Grilled Hairtail

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 30 minutes salting · Cook: 10 minutes · Total: About 55 minutes · Serves 3–4

Ingredients

The fish

  • 700 g hairtail (about 2 fish), cut into 10 cm fingers, or whole if your grill is long enough

The cure

  • 1.5 tsp coarse salt (about 7 g)
  • A grind of black pepper

The finish and table

  • Neutral oil for the grate (about 10 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g), sliced scallion (about 10 g), lemon wedge or splash of vinegar (about 10 ml)
  • Steamed rice (about 500 g cooked), kimchi (#B01), and a light soy-vinegar dip (#SA05)

Instructions

  1. Fingers skin-down on the oiled moderate grate, 3–4 minutes undisturbed. The skin will tighten, silver going pewter, then blister gold at the edges. Checkpoint — the skin lifts clean where it crisped and stays put where it has not — work from the done end with the spatula, not all at once. The flesh at the cut ends turns opaque upward from the skin.
  2. One gentle flip, 2–3 minutes more, off the fire when the flesh at the thickest finger's center is opaque and flakes at a fork's nudge. Checkpoint — a test finger pulled apart: opaque through, sweet and firm-juicy, the spine releasing from the meat with no resistance. Hairtail's flesh is dense but delicate — it will not forgive much past this point, but it does not need to; the clock is short.
  3. Platter, sesame and scallion over, lemon beside, and the eating is handwork: lift the spine free of each finger, the meat in one or two pieces. Checkpoint — eating test: crisp skin and sweet white meat in the same bite, the soy-vinegar dip for the pieces that want brightening. If the skin is leathery instead of crisp, the finger went on wet — the drying law is the record.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Salted fingers hold 24 hours refrigerated. Cooked, 2 days — the meat lifts off the spine in clean strips for rice bowls or gimbap filling. - Variations: a light dusting of flour before the grill (the Busan street style) for a crisper coat at the cost of some skin's shine; the same cure on mackerel (#SF07) at a longer clock; the gochujang swipe at the end for the spicy table.
