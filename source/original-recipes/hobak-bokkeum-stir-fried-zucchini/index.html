**Stream: heritage — the honest vegetable bowl.** Hobak bokkeum is the banchan that proves the category's quiet point: not every bowl ferments or braises — some are just a vegetable, a hot pan, garlic, and sesame, done in fifteen minutes. Korean zucchini (a tender, pale-green variety) or the standard kind, cut into half-moons, seared until the edges gold and the centers stay crisp-shouldered, dressed while hot. It is the vegetable that keeps the grill table honest, the bowl that makes the table feel complete without announcing itself.

The zucchini

The seasoning

- **Salt the half-moons 10 minutes and squeeze them gently** — the same weep-and-press law as the cucumber (#B07). Zucchini is 95 percent water; un-salted, it steams to gray mush in the pan. Salted and lightly pressed, it sears gold and holds shape. This one step is the entire difference between the restaurant bowl and the sad one. - Keep the pieces at an honest 1 cm — thinner cooks to nothing, thicker never gives up its water.

Hobak Bokkeum Stir-Fried Zucchini

Prep: 8 minutes · Cook: 7 minutes · Total: About 15 minutes · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The zucchini

  • 500 g zucchini (2 medium), cut into 1 cm half-moons

The seasoning

  • 1 tsp coarse salt (about 5 g), for the 10-minute draw
  • 1.5 tbsp neutral oil (about 18 g)
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), sliced
  • 1 tsp soy sauce (5 ml)
  • A pinch of salt (about 1 g) and ground pepper
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced; a pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional

Instructions

  1. Oil at medium-high, garlic in for 30 seconds of blonding, then the pressed zucchini in one layer as much as the pan allows — 2 minutes undisturbed for the first side. Checkpoint — the cut faces gold against the green skin, and the pan sizzles steadily rather than hissing out steam — the draw did its work. If the pan floods with liquid, the press was skipped; pour it off, accept a softer bowl, salt earlier next time.
  2. Toss or turn, 2–3 minutes more — the zucchini should stay crisp-shouldered with gilded edges, not collapse. Checkpoint — the bend test: a half-moon bends with a spine of resistance. Fully limp zucchini is over; this bowl's identity is the last of the crunch.
  3. Off the heat: soy, pepper, sesame oil, seeds, scallion, gochugaru if using — the residual heat blooms them without cooking them away. Checkpoint — the eating test with rice: sesame-garlic first, the zucchini's sweet green core, a clean finish. It should taste like the vegetable decided to dress up, not like dressing looking for a vegetable.

Troubleshooting

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Best the day it's made; acceptable at a day refrigerated, re-panned for 60 seconds. The raw salted half-moons hold half a day in the fridge, ready to finish. - Variations: matchstick carrot added at the sear for color; a spoon of ssamjang (#SA01) thinned into the pan for the deep-flavored fork; the same build on mushrooms or snap peas carries the method anywhere.
