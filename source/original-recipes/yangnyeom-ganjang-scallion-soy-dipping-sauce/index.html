Korean · Easy · Makes about 120 ml

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the shavings' bowl.** Yangnyeom ganjang — seasoned soy — is the scallion-forward soy dip that the fast records run on: chadolbaegi (#M07) is nearly defined by it, and the clean-lane shrimp (#SF02) and the squid (#SF04) all reach for it. Where gireumjang (#SA02) amplifies fat, this bowl cuts it: soy's salt, vinegar's edge, scallion's bite, sesame's round finish, chili optional. Two builds from one ingredient list — the quick raw mix and the ten-minute bloomed version — and the difference between them is worth tasting once side by side.

- **Decide the build.** The raw mix is brighter and greener — five minutes, scallion crunchy. The bloomed build takes the scallion whites and garlic through warm oil for a minute and pours the sizzling oil over the rest — softer, deeper, the restaurant's version. Same bowl, two registers; the raw is the default, the bloomed is the date-night. - Slice the scallions thin and evenly — they ARE the sauce, and thick rounds read as salad garnish instead of seasoning.

Yangnyeom Ganjang Scallion-Soy Dipping Sauce

Prep: 8 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 8 minutes, or 15 with the bloomed build · Makes about 120 ml

Ingredients

The base (both builds)

  • 4 tbsp soy sauce (60 ml)
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar (30 ml)
  • 1 tsp honey or sugar (about 5 g)
  • 4 scallions (about 60 g), finely sliced, whites and greens separated
  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (about 6 g) or 1 sliced fresh chili (about 10 g)
  • 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g), lightly crushed
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)

The bloomed build only

  • 1 clove garlic (about 4 g), sliced
  • 1 tsp neutral oil (about 4 g)

Instructions

  1. **Raw build:** soy, vinegar, and honey stirred clear; scallions (all of them), gochugaru, sesame seeds, and the sesame oil folded in. Done, and better after ten minutes' rest while the scallion bleeds its green into the soy. Checkpoint — the taste: salt first, vinegar's snap right behind, scallion's onion-sweetness, sesame's finish, the gochugaru coloring the whole thing warm. On a piece of fatty brisket shaving (#M07), the fat should taste cleaner after the dip, not greasier — that is the bowl's whole function.
  2. **Bloomed build:** neutral oil and the garlic slices at medium-low until the garlic is blond; the scallion whites and gochugaru in for 30 seconds just to wake; pour the sizzling oil over the soy-vinegar-honey base with the scallion greens and sesame already in it. It crackles, blooms, and goes quiet. Checkpoint — the bloomed bowl is rounder — the raw edge of vinegar and scallion cooked a half-step toward sweet — and it keeps a day longer. Both bowls should be assertive; a shy yangnyeom ganjang is soy sauce with regrets.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Raw build keeps 3 days (add the greens at serving); bloomed keeps 5. Both hold the flavors that make grilled meat interesting. - Variations: the onion-fork (#SA05); a pinch of black pepper for the beef records; a teaspoon of the gochujang (#SA03) swirled in for a bowl that leans hot — labeled as the hybrid it is.
