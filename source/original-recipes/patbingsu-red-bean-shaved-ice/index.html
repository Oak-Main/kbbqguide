Patbingsu Red-Bean Shaved Ice

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 (one shared mountain)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the summer grill's cold ending.** Patbingsu is the shared mountain of shaved ice, sweetened red bean, condensed milk, and fruit that ends a summer grill meal the way the hotteok (#D01) ends a winter one: with the whole table around one bowl and four long spoons. The cafe-era versions run elaborate; the home version this record builds is the honest core — ice, pat, milk, a few good toppings — and the technique is entirely in the ice and the bean.

- **Make the pat ahead** — the day before is ideal. Simmer the soaked beans to fully soft (a bean crushes to paste between fingers), drain, and stir in the sugar and salt off the heat; it thickens as it cools to a spoonable, spoon-stands-in-it spoonable paste. Half-mash: some whole beans left, the texture's interest. - Freeze your ice in advance if you are making it: plain water frozen in a shallow tray shaves better than bagged cubes. - Chill the bowls — everything about this dish is temperature, and a warm bowl costs you five minutes of mountain.

Patbingsu Red-Bean Shaved Ice

Prep: 20 minutes (plus freezing for home shavers) · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: About 30 minutes, assembled at the table · Serves 4 (one shared mountain)

Ingredients

The red bean (pat — or the honest shortcut)

  • 200 g dried red beans (pat), 100 g sugar, a pinch of salt, water to cover — simmered 60–90 minutes to soft, then drained and half-mashed (the traditional sweet bean, best made a day ahead)
  • Or 400 g canned sweet red beans (pat in a can, the Korean market's), lightly crushed with a spoon

The ice

  • 4 cups shaved ice (about 600 g): a shaved-ice machine, a food processor in pulses, or two trays of ice crushed in a towel with a rolling pin then pulsed — the target is snow, not cubes

The toppings

  • 4 tbsp sweetened condensed milk (about 80 g)
  • 1 banana, sliced (about 100 g); 100 g strawberries or kiwi; a handful of crispy rice cereal or crushed walnuts (about 20 g); 8 small mochi balls (about 60 g), optional
  • Injeolmi powder or roasted soybean powder (about 10 g), optional, the café's signature dust

Instructions

  1. Shave the ice into a chilled wide bowl — a snow mound, higher at the center, worked quickly (shaved ice melts while you look at it). Checkpoint — the ice is snow-flaked, not granular — it should compress lightly under a spoon and hold the mound's shape. Coarse granita-textured ice is the difference between patbingsu and a snow cone, and the difference is the whole dessert.
  2. The toppings arranged in wedges on the snow — the bean in a fat stripe down the mountain, fruit wedges around, mochi and crunch scattered, the condensed milk zigzagged over everything, the injeolmi powder dusted last. Checkpoint — the visual is the recipe's second half: a striped, crowning mountain, every topping visible, assembled in under three minutes from the first shave (the clock is real — the mountain loses a millimeter a minute at room temperature).
  3. To the table immediately, four long spoons, everyone digging from their own side. Checkpoint — the eating test: the first spoonful should carry ice, bean, condensed milk, and fruit together — cold, sweet, creamy, the bean's earthy depth under the milk's sweetness. The mountain collapses as you eat, the toppings avalanche into the melting snow, and the bowl's last puddle — bean, milk, meltwater — is drunk or fought over. That collapse is the dish's design, not its failure.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCauseFix
Ice crunchy, not snowyCubes not flakesFood processor pulses; shaved-ice machine
Mountain collapsed on arrivalSlow assembly or warm bowlChilled bowl, toppings pre-cut, three minutes
Bean watery and thinUnder-cooked or over-drainedSimmer to full softness; drain but keep a spoon of liquid
All sweetness, no depthCondensed milk heavy, bean lightMore bean per spoonful — the milk is the garnish, not the base

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The pat keeps a week refrigerated and improves; everything else is assembled at the moment. There is no leftover patbingsu — there is only the empty bowl. - Variations: the injeolmi powder's café classic; green tea powder dusted with red bean; the fruit-swap of the season; a scoop of ice cream buried in the mountain center, the summer festival's extravagance.
