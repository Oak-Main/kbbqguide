Tomato-Onion Sesame Salad

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the summer garden bowl.** The simplest bowl on the fresh list: ripe tomato and sweet onion in a soy-sesame dressing, the garden's summer answer to the pickle plate. It is the tomato plant's one traditional seat at the Korean table — arriving with summer and the garden, staying the season — and the bowl that best accompanies the unmarinated grill records (#M06, #M10), where a clean, juicy, savory-sweet salad is exactly the meat's equal.

Tomato-Onion Sesame Salad

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes, best within 2 hours · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The vegetables

  • 3 ripe but firm tomatoes (about 450 g), cut into thick wedges
  • 1/2 sweet onion (about 75 g), sliced paper-thin, soaked 10 minutes in cold water

The dressing

  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp honey (about 5 g)
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • Black pepper; 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • A pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional
  • 1 scallion or a few chives (about 10 g), sliced

Instructions

  1. The dressing whisked — soy, honey, vinegar, sesame oil — and tasted: it should run lighter than the cabbage family's dressings (#F05), soy present but polite. Checkpoint — the spoon test: bright, mild-savory, the sesame forward. The tomato brings its own acid and sugar; the dressing frames it.
  2. Wedges and onion folded in with spoons — one gentle turn, the tomatoes bruise at the second — pepper, sesame, scallion over. Checkpoint — the eating test: the wedge's cold burst, the onion's sweet crunch, the dressing's savory line through it. Beside jumulleok (#M06) — plain seared beef — this bowl is the plate's entire garnish department, and it is enough.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Eats within 2 hours; the dressed tomato keeps nothing. Dressing keeps a week. - Variations: cucumber wedges alongside the tomato; a few drops of yuja (#F05's jar) for the citrus fork; the same dressing over cold blanched green beans carries the method into autumn.
