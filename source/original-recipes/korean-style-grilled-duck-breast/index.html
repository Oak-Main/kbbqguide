**Stream: contemporary.** Duck is the guest at this table, not a native — you will not find it on the old grill lists — but the Korean pantry fits it like it was invited: duck's rich fat wants sesame, soy, garlic, and ginger, and the sharp fresh banchan cut it exactly where it needs cutting. This record runs a skin-on breast the French way, cross-hatched and rendered slow, then finished in the Korean register with a soy-ginger brush and a scallion-sesame finish. Written honestly: a modern bridge, and a good one.

Korean-Style Grilled Duck Breast

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 30 minutes salting · Cook: 15 minutes · Total: About 1 hour including rest · Serves 3–4

Ingredients

The duck

  • 2 duck breasts, skin on, about 350 g each, room temperature

The salt and the brush

  • 1 tsp coarse salt (about 6 g) and black pepper, for the skin
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated; 1 thumb ginger (about 10 g), grated

The finish and table

  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g), 2 scallions (about 30 g) finely sliced
  • Steamed rice (about 400 g cooked), sangchu geotjeori (#F03) or minari muchim (#F04) — the sharp fresh salad is not optional here, kimchi (#B01) beside

Instructions

  1. **Cold pan, skin down.** The one instruction that sounds wrong and is right: the breast goes into a cold pan or onto a grill grate set at its lowest, skin down, and the heat comes up under it. Render 8–10 minutes at gentle heat, pouring off the fat as it pools (keep it — duck fat is this record's second product). Checkpoint — the skin should go from pale to deep gold slowly, the crosshatch opening as the fat renders out of it. No spitting violence: if the pan roars, the heat is too high and the skin browns before the fat has left — lower it and pour off the fat.
  2. **Flip to the flesh side, 2–3 minutes only**, brushing the cooked flesh face with the sauce as it comes off the heat. Duck breast's doneness is medium — the center rosy at 54–56 °C on a thermometer, or the feel of the base of your thumb when your thumb touches your ring finger. Checkpoint — the flesh face should be just colored and glazed, not crusted. Overcooked duck is the record's only real failure; a rosy center is correct here in a way it never is for chicken (#M16, #M17) — duck is served medium at every table that knows it.
  3. **Rest 8 minutes — the full eight.** Duck carries hard, and a breast cut at the pan bleeds its juice onto the board; a breast cut at eight keeps it. Then slice across the breast, on the bias, into thick coins, skin up. Sesame and scallion over, the rest of the brush sauce spooned around. Checkpoint — eating test, with the sharp salad in the same bite: crisp, fully rendered skin; rosy, silky-fatty flesh; soy-ginger glaze; then the geotjeori's vinegar cutting clean. Rich, then reset — that rhythm is the whole plate.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Raw breasts salted keep 24 hours. Cooked, 3 days sliced — cold duck coins over rice with the sauce, or a ssam with perilla and a swipe of ssamjang (#SA01) where the fusion lane lives. - Variations: a gochujang-honey brush for the spicy fork; the same treatment on duck legs runs twice the render time; the reserved fat in place of sesame oil in the scallion dip (#SA04).
