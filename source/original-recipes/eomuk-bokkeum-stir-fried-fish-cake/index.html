Eomuk Bokkeum Stir-Fried Fish Cake

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the market stall's home life.** Eomuk — Korean fish cake, the pressed sheets and tubes sold at every market's fish-cake stall — is famous for its soup, but the stir-fried banchan is the home table's quieter use: sheets sliced and tossed in a hot pan with soy, sugar, garlic, and gochugaru until the edges catch and glaze. It is five ingredients, eight minutes, and the bowl that tastes like a Korean market's afternoon.

Quantities live in the recipe card.

The fish cake

The stir-fry

- **Rinse the fish cake once under running water** — a quick pass, ten seconds. It lifts the surface oil from the packaging (fish cake is pre-fried) and lets the seasonings reach the fish cake instead of sliding on its film. Pat roughly dry. - Slice everything first; the stir-fry itself is eight minutes start to finish with no pauses.

Eomuk Bokkeum Stir-Fried Fish Cake

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 18 minutes · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The fish cake

  • 300 g Korean fish cake (eomuk), any mix of sheets and tubes, sliced into bite pieces

The stir-fry

  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (about 12 g)
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), sliced
  • 1/2 onion (about 75 g), sliced
  • 1 carrot (about 60 g), thin half-moons (optional)
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1 tbsp corn syrup or honey (about 20 g)
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g)
  • A splash of water (about 30 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml), 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g), 2 scallions (about 30 g) cut into batons, a pinch of ground pepper

Instructions

  1. Oil and garlic at medium-high until the garlic barely blonds, onion and carrot in, 90 seconds of high-heat softening — the vegetables should stay crisp-shouldered, not slack. Checkpoint — the pan smells of toasted garlic and sweet onion; the edges of the onion go translucent while its core stays white.
  2. Fish cake in, stirred 2 minutes — it will soften a touch, flex, and its cut edges will sear lightly where they touch the pan. Checkpoint — the fish cake's surface changes from glossy-oily to matte and it bends without breaking — it is ready to take the sauce.
  3. Soy, corn syrup, gochugaru, and the splash of water in, tossed every 30 seconds for 2–3 minutes as the liquid reduces and glosses everything. Checkpoint — the pan is nearly dry with a glossy film on every piece, the fish cake's edges caramelized in spots, the gochugaru's color even across the bowl. Off the heat: sesame oil, scallions, sesame. The eating test — a piece straight from the pan — should be sweet-savory with the fish cake's distinctive bounce and the scallion still raw-bright.

Troubleshooting

Burnt garlic bitternessGarlic on too high heatBlond at 30 seconds, everything else in fast

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 3 days refrigerated, honestly excellent cold in a lunchbox with rice; refresh in a pan for 90 seconds. - Variations: gochujang (a teaspoon) for the red-glaze fork; onion and jalapeño rounds for the drinking table; the same fish cake, unseasoned, into the soup pot with kelp stock is its other career.
