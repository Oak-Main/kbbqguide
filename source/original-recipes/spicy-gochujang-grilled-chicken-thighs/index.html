Spicy Gochujang Grilled Chicken Thighs

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant, the yangnyeom chicken lane.** The spicy fork of dak bulgogi: boneless thighs in a gochujang marinade, grilled to a red-brown gloss. Chicken and gochujang are one of the great pairings of the table — the paste's ferment needs a fat to ride, and chicken fat rides it cleanly. This record runs whole butterflied thighs rather than slices (the paste deserves a thick piece to glaze), and the grill boss's job is the classic two-faces problem: color the outside, cook the center, no black on either.

- **Even thickness is the technique.** A butterfly and a light pound to 1.5 cm means the skin crisps in the same time the center cooks — a thigh thick at one end gives you raw center and black glaze simultaneously. - **Marinate 4–12 hours.** Chicken takes the paste's salt and heat slower than thin slices do; the long window is the flavor, and the skin barely notices (skin protects). - Medium fire planned, with a cool corner: the gochujang sugars and the rendering skin fat both want flare-up insurance.

Spicy Gochujang Grilled Chicken Thighs

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 4–12 hours marinating · Cook: 14 minutes at the table · Total: About 30 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The chicken

  • 900 g boneless, skin-on chicken thighs, butterflied to an even 1.5 cm (skin on renders and crisps; skinless works, glaze harder)

The marinade

  • 3 tbsp gochujang (about 60 g)
  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (about 6 g) — the heat dial
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp honey (about 30 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 5 cloves garlic (about 20 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 10 g), grated
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce ssam, cold oi muchim (#B07), kimchi (#B01), pickled radish cubes

Instructions

  1. Skin side down first on a medium fire — the skin renders slowly into its own fat bath and crisps while the paste on the meat side stays safe above it. Six to eight minutes, undisturbed, while the fat builds. Checkpoint — the skin releases on its own when it is deep golden and crisp; if it is still welded, it is not done. Never force the flip early.
  2. Flip to the paste side, 4–5 minutes. The glaze sets in the rendered fat's heat; watch the edges, where it blackens first. Any flare-up: move the piece to the cool corner for 30 seconds, back to finish. Checkpoint — pull when the center of a test piece is opaque and its juices run clear — no pink, chicken's absolute rule — and the glaze is brick-glossy, charred only at the very fat edge.
  3. Rest 3 minutes, scissors into strips across the thigh, sesame over, platter. Checkpoint — eating test: crisp skin snap, then the juicy paste-glazed meat, heat blooming after the sweet — and the cold cucumber between bites resetting the palate. That triangle is the record.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated thighs keep 24 hours. Cooked, 3 days — cold strips over hot rice, or chopped into a spicy chicken fried rice with the leftover glaze. - Variations: the soy-pear fork (#M16); the same marinade on wings, double-glazed in the last minutes, is the tabletop's snack lane; thinned and brushed on the shrimp record (#SF01) for the spicy table.
