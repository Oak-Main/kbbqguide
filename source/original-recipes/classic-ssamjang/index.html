Classic Ssamjang

Korean · Easy · Makes about 250 g (a table's bowl)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the table's oldest assembly.** Ssamjang — literally "wrap sauce" — is the bowl that makes a grill table a ssam table: doenjang and gochujang in one paste, sweetened, studded with aromatics, sitting between the meat and the lettuce with an authority no bottled sauce matches. It is less a recipe than a ratio with every household's thumbprint on it; this record is the classic center from which the variations swing. Every meat record on this site points here, and the bowl takes ten minutes.

Classic Ssamjang

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes (raw blend) or 5 (cooked build) · Total: 10 minutes · Makes about 250 g (a table's bowl)

Ingredients

The base

  • 3 tbsp doenjang (about 60 g)
  • 1.5 tbsp gochujang (about 30 g)
  • 1 tbsp honey or corn syrup (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g), lightly crushed

The aromatics (the part that separates table-made from jarred)

  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated or minced to a near-paste
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), finely sliced
  • 1/2 Asian pear or apple (about 75 g), grated — the traditional sweetener and the bowl's secret freshness
  • A splash of mirin or water (about 15 ml), to loosen

Instructions

  1. Blend the base: doenjang, gochujang, honey, sesame oil, worked to one uniform rust color. Checkpoint — no light-brown streaks; one paste.
  2. The pear in with its juice, the splash of mirin, then garlic, scallions, sesame. Fold to a spreadable paste — looser than jam, thicker than a dressing; it must cling to a lettuce leaf without pooling. Checkpoint — the raw-build taste test on the leaf: salty-fermented depth, pear's lift, garlic's bite, the scallion still crisp. This is the bowl.
  3. **The cooked build (optional, for a mellower bowl):** the blended paste simmered in a lightly oiled pan 3–4 minutes with a splash of water — the garlic rounds, the gochujang's raw edge cooks off, and the sauce keeps a day longer. The raw bowl is brighter; the cooked bowl is deeper; both are classic, and the choice is the table's. Checkpoint — the cooked build darkens a shade and smells toasted rather than sharp — pull it before the sugars catch (medium-low, stirring, no crisping).

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Raw build keeps 3–4 days refrigerated; the cooked build a week. The bowl actually improves overnight as the pear and garlic marry — make it the morning of the grill. - Variations: a spoon of crushed walnuts (the picnic build); chopped kimchi brine-folded for the funk table; a teaspoon of miso in place of a third of the doenjang for a sweeter, rounder bowl — labeled as the fusion fork.
