Yakgwa Honey-Ginger Cookies

Korean · Medium · Makes about 24 cookies

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the oldest sweet on the table.** Yakgwa — "medicine cookie," named for the era when honey was the pharmacy — is a fried pastry of wheat, sesame oil, honey, ginger, and a splash of rice wine, soaked in a hot ginger-honey syrup after its fry. It is the Korean table's oldest continuous confection, the royal and the temple and the holiday table's sweet, and its craft is a paradox worth learning: a dough so tender it must rest to be handled, fried gently in oil that never runs hot, then drowned in syrup until it turns translucent-gold. The cookie that results keeps for weeks and tastes deeper on day three.

- **Mix the dough as little as possible.** Flour, salt, ginger, sesame seeds whisked; the sesame oil rubbed in with your fingertips to a sandy meal (the fat-coating that blocks gluten), then honey and rice wine stirred in just until the dough clumps — it will look too dry, then suddenly come together. Press it into a ball, wrap, and rest 1 hour at room temperature. The rest relaxes the little gluten that formed and redistributes the honey — a dough fried without the rest is tough, and tough yakgwa is a failed yakgwa. **Checkpoint —** the rested dough is soft, faintly glossy, and smells of sesame and honey; it bends without cracking when folded slowly. - **Cut the syrup's plan:** the syrup is heated to a bare simmer while the cookies fry — the cookies must go into it hot-from-the-oil (see Method), so the syrup's timing is the fry's timing.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The dough: minimally mixed, rested an hour, rolled thick, docked twice.
  • The fry: stage one 130 °C to cooked-through-and-pale, stage two 170 °C to golden in 90 seconds.
  • The syrup: simmered with ginger, the hot cookies bathed as they leave the oil.
  • The finish: cooled to translucent-amber, glossy, tender-firm; best at days 2–5 of three weeks.

Finish & Plate

Stacked loose on a plate with the syrup's ginger threads over, tea or sikhye (#D05) beside. Three cookies a person is the ceremonial serving; the tin on the counter is the weeks-long reality.

Yakgwa Honey-Ginger Cookies

Prep: 30 minutes, plus 1 hour resting · Cook: 40 minutes (two-stage fry) · Total: About 2 hours 20 minutes · Makes about 24 cookies

Ingredients

The dough

  • 250 g all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)
  • 1/4 tsp ground ginger (about 1 g), plus 1 tbsp fresh grated ginger (about 8 g)
  • 50 ml toasted sesame oil
  • 80 ml honey
  • 2 tbsp rice wine or soju (30 ml) — the traditional tenderizer
  • 2 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 16 g), optional, in the dough

For shaping

  • About 60 g additional flour, for the board and stamp

The syrup

  • 120 ml honey
  • 60 g sugar
  • 80 ml water
  • 6 slices ginger (about 15 g)
  • A pinch of salt (about 1 g)

The fry

  • About 800 ml neutral oil, for the two-stage fry

Instructions

  1. **Roll thick, cut, and stamp.** The dough rolled between floured parchment to 1 cm — thick, by cookie standards — and cut into shapes: the traditional flower stamp pressed into rounds, or simple diamonds for the honest home version. Dock each cookie twice with a fork (steam vents — the fry puffs them) and rest the cut cookies 15 minutes while the oil heats. Checkpoint — each cut cookie holds its edge cleanly; a ragged edge means the dough warmed too much — back to the fridge for ten minutes.
  2. **The two-stage fry — infuse, then color.** Stage one: oil at 130 °C (gentle — a cookie dropped in takes 4–5 seconds to bubble to the surface), the cookies in small batches, 12–15 minutes, turned once — they barely color, and the low heat cooks the starch through while the sesame oil's flavor soaks the interior. Stage two: the oil raised to 170 °C, the same cookies returned for 90 seconds — they turn deep gold-brown and the surface crisps. Checkpoint — stage one's cookie is pale-blond and cooked through (break one: no raw dough at the center), and stage two's is golden with a light crust and a delicate, almost pastry-flaky interior. The double fry exists because a single hot fry colors the outside long before the honey-laden interior cooks — the honey burns, the center stays raw — and there is no single-temperature workaround.
  3. **The syrup bath — while hot.** The simmered syrup taken off the heat, and the hot-from-the-oil cookies slid straight in, 30–60 seconds a side, then lifted to a rack over a tray. They drink the syrup as they cool, turning from golden to translucent amber, the surface going glassy. Checkpoint — the cooled cookie's test: glossy, translucent at the edges, tender-firm, the ginger-honey flavor soaked fully through — the first bite should explain the "medicine" in the name to anyone who thinks it a metaphor. A syrup-bathed yakgwa keeps three weeks and is best at days 2–5.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The keeper of the dessert list: three weeks airtight, improving through the first five days as the syrup soaks. Make a double batch when the honey jar is generous. - Variations: pressed with a pine-nut half before frying; a cinnamon thread in the syrup; the temple-style thick diamond, cut 2 cm and fried longer at the low stage — chewier, the meditation-hall register.
