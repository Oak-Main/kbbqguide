Baechu Geotjeori Fresh Kimchi

Korean · Easy · Serves 6–8 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the unfermented same-day kimchi.** Geotjeori is kimchi's answer to right now: napa cabbage salted just enough to soften, dressed in the full kimchi paste, and on the table within the hour — no ferment, no jar, no waiting. It is sweeter and greener than the fermented bowl (#B01), built for the day the jar runs empty or the grill was decided this morning. This is also the record for the first-time kimchi maker: every flavor of the tradition, none of the calendar.

Quantities live in the recipe card.

The cabbage and salt

The dressing

- **A short salt, not a brine.** Toss the cut cabbage with the salt, rest 20 minutes, and it weeps enough to wilt the leaves a half-step while staying crisp — geotjeori's whole texture. Rinse lightly, squeeze firmly by the handful (squeezing also breaks the leaves' cell walls so the dressing soaks in — this dish's one physical technique), and drain. - Build the dressing while the cabbage sits.

Baechu Geotjeori Fresh Kimchi

Prep: 20 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 20 minutes, best within 3 hours · Serves 6–8 as banchan

Ingredients

The cabbage and salt

  • 1/2 large head napa cabbage (about 1 kg), cut into 4 cm lengths
  • 1 tbsp coarse salt (about 15 g), plus more to taste

The dressing

  • 3 tbsp gochugaru (about 18 g)
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce (15 ml) — or 1 tbsp soy sauce for the vegan table
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 tsp sugar or honey (about 5 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), sliced; 1/2 Asian pear (about 100 g), in fine matchsticks; 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g)

Instructions

  1. Gochugaru, fish sauce, garlic, ginger, and sugar worked to a paste, loosened with a tablespoon of the cabbage's rinse water; pear matchsticks and scallion folded through. Checkpoint — the paste tastes complete and slightly over-strong (sweet-savory-hot with a raw gochugaru edge) — the cabbage's water rounds it within the hour.
  2. The squeezed cabbage and the dressing tossed together by hand, massaged for a full minute so every leaf's surface takes the paste, sesame oil and seeds last. Checkpoint — the bowl test, ten minutes after mixing: crisp, wet-crunchy leaves, the dressing clinging rather than pooling, the flavor fully arrived — bright, sweet-hot, green. No sourness, and none expected: geotjeori's identity is the absence of the ferment.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Best within 3 hours, acceptable at a day refrigerated, wrong at two — this is a make-and-eat bowl. - Variations: cucumber-geotjeori (#B03's instant cousin); a spoon of the leftover dressing makes instant radish geotjeori; the fermented commitment is B01.
