Injeolmi Toast with Ice Cream

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 (8 toasts)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: contemporary — the café's grill-adjacent dessert.** Injeolmi toast is the modern Korean café's signature sweet: mochi rice cakes pressed crisp in a buttered pan, stacked on toast, dusted with roasted soybean powder and honey, and — the full build — crowned with a scoop of ice cream that melts down through the warm layers. It is the sweet that tastes like Seoul's dessert cafés, born decades after every other record on this dessert list, and it is here because the grill meal's table deserves one hot-cold showpiece that takes twenty minutes.

Injeolmi Toast with Ice Cream

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 20 minutes · Serves 4 (8 toasts)

Ingredients

The components

  • 8 small rectangle injeolmi rice cakes (about 240 g), or fresh mochi cut to size — the refrigerated Korean-market kind, not frozen
  • 4 thick slices milk bread or brioche (about 200 g)
  • 2 tbsp butter (about 30 g)
  • 4 tbsp roasted soybean powder (injeolmi powder / konggaru, about 30 g)
  • 3 tbsp honey or condensed milk (about 60 g)
  • 4 small scoops vanilla or milk-flavored ice cream (about 200 g)
  • A pinch of fine salt (about 1 g), over the toast

Instructions

  1. Toast the bread golden in half the butter — pan or grill grate, the grill's last heat is the tradition's own trick — and hold it warm. Checkpoint — deep golden and butter-crisp at the face, still soft at the center — thick bread's job is to be a warm, crisp platform.
  2. The rice cakes into the remaining butter at medium heat, pressed flat with the spatula (each pressed to half its height), 90 seconds a side to a blistered, crisped, lightly golden skin with the interior gone stretchy-soft. Checkpoint — the pressed cake's surface is blistered and toasted, and a test pull stretches — crisp outside, mochi inside, the dessert's central texture in one piece.
  3. The build, per plate: toast, a drizzle of honey, two pressed rice cakes over, a heavy dust of the soybean powder (the flavor that names the dessert — nutty, roasted, faintly sweet), another honey drizzle, the ice cream scoop crowning, a final dust over the whole plate. Checkpoint — the eating test, immediately, while the temperatures still oppose: the ice cream melting into the powder's dust, the rice cake's stretch, the toast's butter-crisp, and the soybean-honey flavor running through all three layers. Ninety seconds of contrast is the dessert's whole window — serve at the pan, not from the kitchen.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Assembles and eats immediately; the components keep separately (rice cakes refrigerated, powder months airtight). The fully-built toast keeps nothing, by design. - Variations: condensed milk in place of honey; a thread of matcha in the powder dust for the café's green build; grilled fruit — the summer market's peaches — laid under the rice cakes for the seasonal fork.
