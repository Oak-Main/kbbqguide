Doenjang-Garlic Moksal Gui

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

**Stream: restaurant.** Moksal — pork collar, the shoulder's marble — is the connoisseur's cut of the tabletop: tender-firm with a fine fat weave that stands up to the grill better than loin ever will. Its natural partner is doenjang, the fermented soybean paste most Western kitchens only meet in jjigae. As a marinade base, doenjang gives pork what gochujang cannot: deep savory funk with almost no sugar, nothing to burn, and a crust that browns like a steak's instead of lacquering. This is the quiet record of the pork list — the one the regulars order.

Doenjang-Garlic Moksal Gui

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 2–4 hours marinating · Cook: 12 minutes at the table · Total: About 30 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The pork

  • 900 g pork collar (moksal) or shoulder, sliced 7 mm thick

The marinade

  • 2 tbsp doenjang (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp mirin or honey (15 ml / about 20 g)
  • 6 cloves garlic (about 24 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 10 g), grated
  • 1/2 onion (about 75 g), grated
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), kimchi (#B01), musaengchae spicy radish (#B08) — the cold, crisp counter this rich cut wants

Instructions

  1. Charcoal or cast-iron at medium-high. The slices go on in a single layer; with almost no sugar in the marinade, the grill boss is building crust, not managing lacquer. First side undisturbed 2–3 minutes. Checkpoint — the smell is roasted soybean and garlic over browning pork — deeper and darker than any other record at this grill. The underside should be crusty brown and release cleanly.
  2. Flip once, 2 minutes more, pull when the meat's juices bead at the surface and the slice is just firm to a fingertip press. Rest the batch 1 minute; moksal carries a little and finishes gently. Checkpoint — test piece: crusty edge, juicy interior with a firm-but-tender chew that loin never gives, and the doenjang's savory depth arriving right behind the pork. No sweetness ahead of the meat — if there is, the honey ran heavy.
  3. Scissors into bite pieces, warm platter, straight into ssam with ssamjang or wrapped plain with rice and a strand of musaengchae in the bite. Checkpoint — the ssam test: the crust and the chew are the record's signature — this should taste like the grown-up table at the restaurant, the one that skipped the sweet marinades on purpose.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCauseFix
Marinade lumpyDoenjang not loosened firstWhisk paste with liquids before aromatics
Too saltySalty doenjang plus soyDrop the soy to 2 tsp; 2-hour marinate
Tough slicesCut with the grain or overcookedAgainst the grain, 7 mm; pull at bead-and-press
Bland crustMarinate too shortFull 2 hours minimum; grate the garlic fine

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated slices keep 24 hours. Cooked, 3 days — cold moksal over rice with a fried egg is the intended leftover, the crust softening into the yolk. - Variations: a teaspoon of gochujang stirred into the marinade warms it toward #M11's lane; the same marinade on chicken thighs (#M17's register); leftover marinade simmered 3 minutes into a spoon-sauce for the platter.
