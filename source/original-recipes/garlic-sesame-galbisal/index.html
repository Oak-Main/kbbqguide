**Stream: restaurant.** Galbisal is the boneless strip off the rib — all of the galbi flavor, none of the bone, and it grills faster than its flanken cousin (#M03). The classic restaurant treatment keeps the marinade short and quiet: garlic, sesame oil, soy, a thread of sweetness, black pepper. The meat is the argument; the marinade is the microphone. This is the record for the table that wants galbi flavor on a weeknight clock, and for the grill boss who is tired of managing sugar on the fire — there is barely any here to burn.

Garlic-Sesame Galbisal

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 2–4 hours marinating · Cook: 10 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The beef

  • 800 g boneless beef short rib (galbisal) or chuck flap, sliced 5 mm against the grain

The marinade

  • 6 cloves garlic (about 24 g), grated
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp toasted sesame oil (about 22 ml)
  • 1 tbsp honey or sugar (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds, plus more to finish (about 8 g total)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), gireumjang (#SA02) as the purist dip, kimchi (#B01)

Instructions

  1. Charcoal or cast-iron at medium-high. The slices go on in a single layer, and because there is almost no sugar, the grill boss's job is color, not lacquer-management: leave them until the edges weep and the underside is browned, about 2 minutes. Checkpoint — the smell should be sesame and garlic over browning beef — a quieter grill smell than the sweet marinades, and the honest one for this cut.
  2. Flip once, 90 seconds more, pull when the raw red is just gone at the center of the slice. Rest the batch 1 minute on a warm plate — galbisal is thin enough that carryover is quick and real. Checkpoint — eating test straight off the grill: beef first, garlic second, sesame after. If you taste marinade before meat, the soy ran heavy — pull back a teaspoon next batch.
  3. Scissors into bite pieces, warm platter, sesame over, ssam station open. The purist dip here is gireumjang (#SA02) — sesame oil, salt, pepper — because it seasons without competing; ssamjang (#SA01) for the bolder table. Checkpoint — the ssam test: leaf, gireumjang, galbisal, fold — and it should taste like the steakhouse version of Korean grill, clean enough that the beef's quality is the headline. Buy the good beef for this one; there is nowhere to hide.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCauseFix
Bland despite marinadeSoy too light or beef wateryFull-salt soy; pat slices dry before marinating
Garlic burned bitterGrated garlic on a too-hot grateMedium-high, not rocket; 2-minute first side
Slices toughCut with the grainRe-slice against the grain; 5 mm max
Marinade pooled wateryOver-marinated or wet beef2–4 hours only; dry the slices first

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated slices keep 24 hours refrigerated. Cooked, 3 days — cold slices over rice with a fried egg and the leftover gireumjang is the intended day-after move. - Variations: a teaspoon of gochugaru in the marinade for a slow heat; the mushroom-soy build (#M02) swaps in cleanly; the bone-in flanken fork (#M03) when the table wants the full ceremony.
