Spicy Grilled Nakji Octopus

Korean · Medium · Serves 3–4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant.** Nakji is the small octopus of the nakji-bokkeum tradition — the fierce stir-fried classic — and this record is that flavor set run over live fire instead of a screaming wok. The technique difference matters more than the flavor one: a whole small octopus (about 300 g each) is too thick to stay tender at grill speed, so it needs either the old-school pounding or a brief pre-simmer before the grill. This record runs the simmer-then-grill path — the home-kitchen-honest one — and the gochujang glaze lands in the final minute, same law as the squid record (#SF04).

- **The pre-simmer is the tenderizing — do not skip it for the fire's sake.** Bring the salted, gingered water to a boil, drop in the octopus, and simmer exactly 4 minutes for small ones. Lift, cool under running water, and drain. The flesh is now tender-set and the grill's job is color and glaze only. Skipping this and grilling raw is how nakji becomes a rubber band. - **Slice after the simmer:** body into wide rings or opened flat into sheets (scored lightly in a crosshatch, the #SF04 law), tentacles in halves lengthwise. Pat thoroughly dry. - Whisk the glaze and park it at the grill station. Fire at medium-high.

Spicy Grilled Nakji Octopus

Prep: 25 minutes, plus 1 hour tenderizing · Cook: 12 minutes · Total: About 1 hour 40 minutes, mostly resting · Serves 3–4

Ingredients

The octopus

  • 2 small whole octopus (about 300 g each), cleaned — or 600 g octopus, tentacles and body separated

The pre-simmer

  • Water to cover, 1 tbsp salt (about 15 g), 3 slices ginger (about 10 g), 2 scallion whites (about 20 g)

The glaze

  • 2 tbsp gochujang (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 500 g cooked), bean sprouts or mu sangchae (about 100 g) for the cold crunch, sesame seeds (about 4 g)

Instructions

  1. Dry slices onto the hot grate in a single layer, 90 seconds undisturbed — you are searing color onto already-cooked octopus, so every second is flavor, not doneness. Checkpoint — char marks on the faces and edges, the scored sheets curling open. The smell is toast over the ocean — clean and sweet.
  2. Flip, brush the seared faces with glaze, 45–60 seconds, second brush-stroke, off the fire. Total grill time under 3 minutes — the simmer did the cooking and the glaze only needs to set. Checkpoint — glossy brick-red slices, char at the edges. The eating test at the grill station: tender-chewy with a snap that yields (nakji's correct texture — firmer than squid, never rubber), sweet-heat over octopus depth.
  3. Platter, sesame over, and the table eats it over rice with the cold crunchy banchan beside. Checkpoint — the bowl test: glaze-slicked nakji over rice with bean sprouts in the same bite — heat, chew, crunch. If every bite is all glaze, the slices were too small; wide pieces carry the balance.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Simmered octopus keeps 2 days refrigerated before grilling — the record's make-ahead is built in. Cooked, 2 days, into fried rice or noodles. - Variations: the no-simmer pounding path (see Note); baby octopus skips the slicing and runs whole on the jjukkumi record (#SF06); the glaze doubles on the squid record (#SF04).
