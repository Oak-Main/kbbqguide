**Stream: contemporary.** Lamb is the newest voice at this grill — absent from the old lists, fully at home in the modern pantry — and it asks for the two pastes at once: doenjang for the savory floor, gochujang for the red heat, honey to hold them together against lamb's big flavor. This is a butterflied shoulder steak record: marinated long, grilled hot in thick slices, served ssam-style with the sharp fresh salads that lamb and the Korean table both love. No lineage claimed — this one is written for the cook who wants the Korean grill's flavors on the meat their butcher is proudest of this week.

- **The two pastes must be worked smooth together before anything else joins** — doenjang and gochujang are both stiff and will not dissolve on their own. Whisk them with the soy, honey, and sesame oil into a uniform rust-colored paste, then the aromatics and the squeezed pear. - **Marinate 8–24 hours, refrigerated.** Lamb shoulder is a thick, working muscle; the long window is not optional, and the pear's enzyme work is doing half the tenderizing. Contemporary Doenjang-Gochujang Lamb Shoulder

Prep: 20 minutes, plus 8–24 hours marinating · Cook: 30 minutes · Total: About 1 hour (marinate ahead) · Serves 4–6

Ingredients

The lamb

  • 1.2 kg butterflied lamb shoulder (or leg steaks cut 2 cm thick), trimmed of thick fat caps but left with a thin fat layer

The marinade

  • 2 tbsp doenjang (about 40 g)
  • 2 tbsp gochujang (about 40 g)
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp honey (about 30 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 6 cloves garlic (about 24 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 15 g), grated
  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 150 g), grated and squeezed nearly dry
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), perilla and lettuce ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), kkaennip jangajji pickled perilla (#B24) or quick-pickled onion, kimchi (#B01)

Instructions

  1. Slice the marinated shoulder across the grain into strips 2 cm wide. Sear in a single layer on the medium-hot zone — lamb's fat wants to flare, so the grill boss stays at the grate with tongs, moving strips to the cool corner when their edges catch. Checkpoint — the strips should sear to a deep brown-red crust with light char at the fat edges, and the smell should be the pastes toasting over rendered lamb fat — rich enough that the table will ask what it is before it is ready.
  2. **Work in batches, brush as each batch finishes:** 2–3 minutes a side for medium-rare to medium (lamb's just-pink center at 57–60 °C is where the juiciness lives; well-done lamb shoulder is a lost cause at any heat), a stroke of the reserved marinade on the hot slices as they come off. Checkpoint — a test strip cut at the grill station: crust, then just-pink juicy center, and the pastes' savory-heat arriving behind the lamb's big flavor rather than on top of it. If the marinade reads louder than the meat, the strips were thin or the marinade thick — the meat is the headline at this table.
  3. Rest the batches 3 minutes on a warm platter as they come off, sesame over, and the platter goes to the table's center for ssam service: perilla leaf, a swipe of ssamjang (#SA01), a hot strip, a piece of pickled perilla or onion, fold, one bite. Checkpoint — the ssam test: the perilla's anise edge against the lamb's richness with the pickled cut through the fat is the record's finished thought — rich, sharp, herbal, one bite. If the wrap collapses into pure richness, the pickle was skipped; it is load-bearing here.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated shoulder keeps 24 hours, freezes 1 month. Cooked, 3 days — leftover slices into a gimbap-style roll or a rice bowl with the last reserved marinade warmed as sauce. - Variations: the same marinade on lamb chops (shorter marinate, 4 hours); beef short rib takes this paste beautifully for a nontraditional weeknight galbi (#M03's cut, this record's flavor); mushroom caps in the reserved marinade grill alongside for the table's vegetarians.
