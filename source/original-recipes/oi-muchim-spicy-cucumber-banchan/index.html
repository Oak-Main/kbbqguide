Oi Muchim Spicy Cucumber Banchan

Korean · Easy · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the instant cucumber bowl.** Oi muchim is cucumber's same-hour answer to kimchi: sliced, salted ten minutes to draw the water, tossed in a gochugaru-garlic dressing and on the table before the rice cools. It is the cold-crunch bowl the spicy records keep calling for — the site's M-records reach for it the way rich tables reach for cold anything — and it is the easiest banchan on this list. No ferment, no cook, one law: salt first, dress second.

Quantities live in the recipe card.

The cucumbers and salt

The dressing

- **The ten-minute salt is the dish.** Toss the sliced cucumbers with the salt and let them weep 10 minutes; the slices bend slightly, the water that would flood your dressing leaves now instead. Pour that water off — do not rinse. - Whisk the dressing while they weep; it takes four of the ten minutes.

Oi Muchim Spicy Cucumber Banchan

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes, best within 2 hours · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Ingredients

The cucumbers and salt

  • 3 small Persian or Kirby cucumbers (about 500 g), sliced into half-moons 5 mm thick
  • 1 tsp coarse salt (about 5 g)

The dressing

  • 1.5 tbsp gochugaru (about 9 g)
  • 1 tsp fish sauce or soy sauce (about 5 ml)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced
  • 1 tsp honey or sugar (about 5 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)

Instructions

  1. The wept cucumbers, squeezed gently by the handful over the sink (one squeeze, not a wring — cucumber should stay crisp, not crushed), into the dressing bowl. Checkpoint — the slices are flexible but still snappy, and the bowl they leave behind holds a clear puddle — that puddle is the dilution you just prevented.
  2. Everything tossed together, taste immediately, and adjust — cucumber wants the dressing slightly stronger than the target bite, because it keeps watering for the next hour. Checkpoint — the eating test two minutes later: cold snap, sweet-heat, garlic hum, sesame finish — the fresh register of #B03's fermented cousin, and the perfect reset bite after gochujang pork (#M13) or lacquered galbi (#M04).

Troubleshooting

Bland at the tableDressed to target, not past itCucumber dilutes — dress a notch strong
Too hot to eat beside spicy mainsGochugaru-heavy dressingHalf the gochugaru; the bowl is a coolant

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Best within 2 hours, edible at a day refrigerated; this is the make-and-eat bowl. - Variations: thin-sliced onion folded in for sweetness; a splash of vinegar to walk it toward the cho-gochujang lane (#SA03); matchstick apple in the dressing for the picnic build.
