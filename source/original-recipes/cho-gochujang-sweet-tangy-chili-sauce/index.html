Cho-Gochujang Sweet-Tangy Chili Sauce

Korean · Easy · Makes about 200 g

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — vinegar's bowl.** Cho-gochujang — "vinegar gochujang" — is the bright member of the dip family: gochujang loosened and sharpened with vinegar and sweetener into a spooning-and-dunking sauce that runs where ssamjang (#SA01) sits. It is the classic partner for raw and chilled things — cold noodles, cucumber, hwe-style seafood — and at the grill table it is the bowl for anything that wants sweetness cut with a real acid line: the shrimp records, the squid (#SF04), skewers (#M18). One bowl, ten minutes, and the vinegar does the transforming.

- **The order: dissolve the sweet in the sour before the paste joins.** Vinegar and honey whisked until clear, then the gochujang worked in — this direction dissolves the paste smoothly; the reverse direction chases lumps. - **Loosen to the target use:** a spooning sauce for noodles and cucumber runs thicker (barely falling from the spoon); a dunking bowl for grilled pieces runs looser (another tablespoon of water). Decide the bowl's job before the last splash. - Taste after the sesame oil, before the build-ins: it should read sweet-and-sour first, fermented-warm second — like a Korean sweet-chili sauce that went to school. Sharper or sweeter is a teaspoon of vinegar or honey away.

Cho-Gochujang Sweet-Tangy Chili Sauce

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 10 minutes · Makes about 200 g

Ingredients

The sauce

  • 3 tbsp gochujang (about 60 g)
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar (30 ml)
  • 1.5 tbsp honey or corn syrup (about 30 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp water (15 ml), plus more to loosen
  • 1 clove garlic (about 4 g), grated
  • 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • 1/2 tsp gochugaru (about 1.5 g), optional heat edge

The classic build-ins (choose to the table)

  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), finely sliced
  • 1/2 cucumber, deseeded and small-diced (about 75 g) — the cold-noodle classic's version
  • Thin slivers of Asian pear (about 40 g) for the raw-seafood lane

Instructions

  1. Vinegar, honey, and water whisked clear; gochujang in, worked to one even red-orange with no dark streaks. Checkpoint — a spoon dragged through leaves a slowly-closing trail — the thickness of thick honey for the noodle build.
  2. Garlic, sesame oil, sesame seeds; the build-ins to the bowl's job; a final taste — the vinegar should be present on the finish of a dipped finger, a clean snap after the sweet. Checkpoint — the eating test on a piece of plain cucumber: snap, sweet, heat hum, acid line. If the cucumber tastes pickled in ten seconds of sitting, the vinegar ran a splash heavy; if it reads as plain gochujang, the vinegar is shy.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 1 week refrigerated and genuinely improves on days two and three as the vinegar mellows into the paste. Cucumber build-ins go in at serving, not storage. - Variations: the raw-seafood build with pear slivers; a spoon over the jjukkumi record (#SF06) instead of the marinade for a lighter tent; thinned further with water and a drop of soy, it dresses the cold noodle bowls and the squid's leftovers.
