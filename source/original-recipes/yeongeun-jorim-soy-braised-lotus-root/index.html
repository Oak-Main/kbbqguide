Yeongeun Jorim Soy-Braised Lotus Root

Korean · Medium · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the elegant braise.** Lotus root, cut into coins, braised in soy and sweetness until the holes fill with glaze and the flesh turns amber-tender: yeongeun jorim is the banchan that looks like architecture and eats like comfort. The coin's lace of holes catches the braise in every bite — the visual is the recipe's promise — and the long braise turns a crunchy raw root into something tender-firm and deeply savory. It is the jorim family's most elegant member, and the patient one: the braise runs half an hour and cannot be rushed.

The braise

- **Peel, cut, and soak at once** — lotus root oxidizes to gray-brown in minutes, and the vinegar-water soak (10 minutes while the rest comes together) keeps the coins snowy and leaches a little starch so the braise glazes instead of gumming. - Check each coin's holes: a quick rinse pokes out the mud that lives in the channels of field-fresh roots.

Yeongeun Jorim Soy-Braised Lotus Root

Prep: 20 minutes · Cook: 30 minutes · Total: About 50 minutes · Serves 4–6 as banchan

Ingredients

The lotus root

  • 600 g fresh lotus root, peeled, cut into 1 cm coins
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar (15 ml), in the soaking water

The braise

  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 2 tbsp water (30 ml)
  • 2 tbsp corn syrup or honey (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), crushed
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml), off heat
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g); 1 dried red chili or a pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced, optional

Instructions

  1. The coins and everything but the sesame finish into a wide pan — soy, water, syrup, mirin, garlic, chili — brought to a simmer, lid on, medium-low. Checkpoint — a gentle bubble, not a boil: the braise should barely tremble. A hard boil breaks the coins' shape before they tender.
  2. **Braise 25–30 minutes, turning the coins once at the halfway mark**, lid on until the last five minutes. The braise reduces slowly while the root drinks. Checkpoint — the doneness read: a paring knife or chopstick slides into a coin's flesh with gentle resistance — tender-firm, never soft, and the thinnest coins' edges have gone translucent amber. The braise by now has reduced to a syrup that pools in the pan's low spots.
  3. **Lid off, the final reduction — 5 minutes**, turning the coins through the thickening syrup every 60 seconds until every face wears glaze and the pan is nearly dry. Off the heat: sesame oil, seeds. Checkpoint — the coin test: bite through and the holes should visibly hold glaze; the flesh gives with a fine, almost crisp-firm grain (lotus keeps a faint snap even fully braised — its signature), savory-sweet through and through.

Troubleshooting

Bland in the centerShort braise or thick coinsEven 1 cm; the full 25–30

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 4–5 days refrigerated and improves for two days as the coins keep drinking — the elegant make-ahead. Serve at room temperature or lightly warmed; the glaze is best not reheated hard. - Variations: the same braise on potato (#B09) and quail eggs (#B19); honey swapped in runs a deeper gold; a garnish of julienned red chili over the coins for the holiday table's full architecture.
