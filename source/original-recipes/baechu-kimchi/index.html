Baechu Kimchi

Korean · Medium · Makes 1 large jar (about 2 kg, a month's table)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the table's anchor.** Every grill table on this site leans on the same bowl: napa cabbage kimchi, the fermented national dish, sour and crunchy and alive, cutting every rich thing the fire produces. This record is the whole-cabbage, cut-at-the-jar method — a proper batch, two hours of honest work and a few days of patience — because the kimchi that answers samgyeopsal (#M10) and dwaeji bulgogi (#M13) should be the real one, not the shriveled retail jar's sad cousin. The process has three crafts in it: the salt that softens the cabbage, the paste that becomes the flavor, and the ferment that turns both into kimchi.

For the jar

- **Salt the cabbage the traditional way, in quarters.** Cut each head lengthwise into quarters through the core (leave the core attached — it holds the quarters together), then salt between every leaf: a pinch of coarse salt at the thick white stem base of each leaf, lighter toward the green tops. Sit the salted quarters in a tub or sink, weighted, 2 hours, turning every 30 minutes. The cabbage weeps, softens, and bends without breaking — that bend is the brine's finish line. Rinse twice in cold water and drain 30 minutes in a colander. **Checkpoint —** a stem folded gently between your fingers bends like a thick noodle, without snapping. Snappy cabbage was under-salted or under-time — salt it longer, there is no other fix. - **Build the paste while the cabbage drains.** Gochugaru, water (or saved brine), grated pear, garlic, ginger, fish sauce, salted shrimp, sugar — stirred into a thick rust-red slurry. Then the radish matchsticks and scallions folded through: they carry the paste between the leaves and become the batch's best bites.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The brine: cabbage bends without snapping, rinsed and drained 30 minutes.
  • The dressing: paste on every stem base, quarters whole, hand-worked.
  • The pack: air pressed out, 5 cm headspace, weight on top, lid loose.
  • The ferment: sour-sweet smell and faint fizz at 1–3 days at 15–20 °C, then refrigerated.

Finish & Plate

Cut a dressed quarter crosswise into bite lengths over the jar (the brine catches the drips), pile into the small bowl with tongs, and set it beside the grill's richest plate. The jar goes back to the fridge the moment the meal ends — the batch's clock never stops, it only slows.

Baechu Kimchi

Prep: 2 hours, hands-on · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 2 hours, then 1–3 days fermenting at 15–20 °C, then the fridge · Makes 1 large jar (about 2 kg, a month's table)

Ingredients

The cabbage and the salt (brining)

  • 2 large heads napa cabbage (about 3 kg total)
  • 1 cup coarse sea salt (about 200 g) for the brine
  • Water as needed

The paste

  • 150 g Korean radish (mu) or daikon, cut into fine matchsticks
  • 4 scallions (about 60 g), cut into 4 cm lengths
  • 1/2 Asian pear or 1 small apple (about 100 g), grated
  • 8 cloves garlic (about 32 g), grated or blitzed
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 25 g), grated
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce (about 45 ml)
  • 2 tbsp salted shrimp (saeujeot, about 30 g), minced — the traditional depth, optional if unfindable
  • 1 cup gochugaru (about 100 g) — mild for the default batch
  • 1 tbsp sugar (about 12 g)
  • 1/2 cup water (120 ml), or the saved brine

For the jar

  • 1 large glass jar or onggi (ceramic crock), clean and dry

Instructions

  1. **Dress the quarters, leaf by leaf, by hand.** Hold a drained cabbage quarter over the paste bowl and work the paste up under each leaf with your gloved or very red bare hands — stem bases get the most, green tops a smear. Do not shred the cabbage: the quarters stay whole, stuffed from the outside in. This hand work is the recipe's heart; a machine has never made this dish. Checkpoint — every leaf's stem carries a visible coat of paste and flecks of radish; the quarter, pressed gently, holds together as one piece.
  2. **Pack the jar tight, in layers.** Fold each dressed quarter over itself (fold the tip toward the core), press it into the jar, and push down hard to squeeze out air pockets — oxygen is the spoilage path, brine is the preservation path. Leave 5 cm of headspace at the top: the ferment expands. Press a clean weight (a small jar, a boiled stone) on top and lid it loosely — never sealed, the ferment needs to breathe or it will bubble over. Checkpoint — after a day, brine rises over the cabbage on its own. If it has not, top up with a little salted water (1 tsp salt per cup) — the vegetables must ferment under liquid, that is the entire safety architecture.
  3. **Ferment warm, then hold cold.** 1–3 days at cool room temperature (15–20 °C — the Korean autumn kitchen's window; warmer runs faster and sharper) until the jar smells sour-sweet and lively and the brine fizzes faintly at a stir, then into the refrigerator, where the ferment slows to a crawl and the flavor deepens for weeks. Checkpoint — the taste at jar-opening day: cabbage still crunchy, paste savory-hot, a clean sourness arriving on the finish — young kimchi, mild and bright. A week in the fridge it rounds; two weeks it sharpens; a month it is the sour, complex elder that stews (#M13's fried-rice day-after) are built for. You are not choosing one kimchi; you are choosing this week's chapter of it.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The jar IS the storage: refrigerated, honestly good for a month and usable to two, riding the arc in the first Note. A second small jar of the same batch, deliberately held at a warmer spot for two extra days, gives the table a sour and a mild bowl simultaneously — the restaurant's own trick. - Variations: the radish batch (#B02) runs the same paste on cubed mu; the fresh, unfermented same-day fork is geotjeori (#B04); cucumber (#B03) takes a faster, brighter version of everything here.
