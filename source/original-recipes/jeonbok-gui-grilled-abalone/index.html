**Stream: restaurant — the coastal luxury.** Abalone is the coastal grill's ceremony dish — the shell itself the vessel, the meat cooked in minutes with nothing but butter, garlic, and the abalone's own juices. Jeonbok gui runs on the same doneness law as squid (#SF04) in reverse miniature: abalone is tender for a very short window and rubber on either side of it. The whole record is that window, plus the ceremony of serving eight shells, still sizzling, to a table that goes quiet.

- **Clean them (if live):** slide a short spoon between meat and shell at the narrow end, work around, and pop the meat free. Cut away the dark viscera and the mouth disc; scrub the foot's edge. The green-gray trim and the liver are delicacies to some tables and discards to others — ask yours; both answers are correct. Return each meat to its shell, fatty foot-side up. - **Score the foot lightly** — a shallow crosshatch, five or six cuts: the scores help the heat in and the baste down, and they telegraph the cooking on the surface. - Fire moderate, pan or grate, with the shells set in a foil cradle or bed of coarse salt so they sit level.

Jeonbok Gui Grilled Abalone

Prep: 20 minutes · Cook: 6 minutes · Total: About 30 minutes · Serves 4 (makes 8)

Ingredients

The abalone

  • 8 live abalone in the shell (about 80–100 g each), or 8 cleaned abalone meats with shells reserved

The baste

  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (about 30 g), melted
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • A pinch of salt (about 1 g)

The finish and table

  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g), finely sliced scallion (about 10 g)
  • Steamed rice (about 300 g cooked), a light lettuce salad, and the table's best drinks — this is a celebration record

Instructions

  1. A spoon of butter into each shell's hollow, the scored abalone over it, shells to the moderate fire in their cradle. Four minutes, no touching. Checkpoint — the butter bubbles around the meat's edge, the scores open pale, and the surface turns from glassy to matte. The smell is butter and sweet ocean.
  2. Baste each meat with the garlicky butter from its own shell, one more minute, off the fire while the foot still has spring at a fingertip press. Checkpoint — the pull test is the record: pressed, the meat springs back slowly (tender-window) — a fast hard snap-back means it tightened past the window; if it never firmed at all, thirty seconds more. The test piece cut thick: resistant-tender, buttery, ocean-sweet — abalone's texture is the dish, and "just right" is a real, findable 60 seconds.
  3. Sesame and scallion over each, the shells straight to the table in the cradle, drinks already poured. Checkpoint — eating test: the whole meat in two or three thick bites off a small fork or the tip of a chopstick's handle, rice after — and the shell's butter-juice pooled beneath is the cook's sip, the oldest gratuity at the coast.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Live abalone keeps 1–2 days in the fridge in a damp cloth over ice — never submerged in fresh water. Cooked, 1 day; this is an eat-in-the-minute record. - Variations: a drop of gochujang butter (#SF12's sauce) in each shell for the spicy fork; the same butter on the oyster record's cups; the cleaned trim and liver into the next morning's jeonbok porridge — the full-use tradition.
