Classic Korean Pear Beef Bulgogi

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the Seoul postwar lane.** This is the bulgogi the world met after the Korean War: thin beef shavings in a sweet pear-onion-soy slurry, grilled until the edges caramelize, with the juice collecting in the pan's moat to be poured over rice. It is not court food, and on this site we do not pretend it is — the scored soy-sesame sheet of neobiani (#M08) is the heritage cousin, and the two are different dishes that share an ancestor. What this version is, unapologetically, is the best of its own lane: real Asian pear in the marinade, beef sliced properly thin, and a tabletop plan so the last batch off the grill is as good as the first. Who cooks what: one grill boss works the grate while the table builds rice bowls. The moat juice is the shared treasure — protect it.

The table

- **Slice first, everything else second.** The marinade's job is to soak a thin sheet, not a steak — at 1–2 mm the pear's enzymes and the soy reach the center in two hours. Freeze the block firm (about 40 minutes) and slice against the grain with your longest knife, one long stroke per slice. - **Grate, don't chop, the aromatics.** Grated pear, onion, garlic, and ginger become a slurry that coats every shaving; chopped pieces sit on top and burn on the grill. Squeeze the grated pear lightly so the marinade is thick, not watery — a wet marinade steams instead of caramelizing. - **Marinate 2 hours minimum, 8 maximum.** Two hours for weeknight dinner is honest; overnight the pear starts breaking the meat down to mush. This lane wants hours in the fridge — that is the law of the Seoul moat, and it is the opposite of the Gwangyang ticket-toss clock (#M06). - Pull the meat from the fridge 20 minutes before grilling. Cold meat on a hot grate equals gray meat.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The slice: 1–2 mm, against the grain, marinated 2–8 hours — never mushy.
  • The grill: single loose layer, flip at the weeping edge, pull when the raw red is just gone.
  • The moat: juice simmered and served over rice, glossy, not syrup.

Finish & Plate

Pile the batches on a warm platter at the table's center, sesame and scallion over. Rice bowls with the juice ladled first, meat over, kimchi (#B01) and pajeori (#F02) within reach of every hand. Ssam leaves and ssamjang (#SA01) for anyone who wants the wrap; the honest Seoul way is the spoon.

Classic Korean Pear Beef Bulgogi

Prep: 20 minutes, plus 2–8 hours marinating · Cook: 10 minutes · Total: About 30 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The beef

  • 700 g beef sirloin or chuck eye, sliced 1–2 mm against the grain (ask the butcher for bulgogi-slice, or freeze 40 minutes and slice yourself)
  • 1 small onion (about 150 g), half grated, half sliced into rings

The marinade

  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 150 g), grated with its juice — do not substitute apple juice
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 15 g), grated
  • 80 ml soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp rice wine or mirin (30 ml)
  • 2 tbsp honey or brown sugar (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), sliced; 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), baechu kimchi (#B01), pajeori scallion salad (#F02), lettuce leaves for ssam, ssamjang (#SA01)
  • Optional moat vegetables: 100 g king oyster mushroom slices, 1/2 carrot (about 60 g), ribboned

Instructions

  1. **Build the slurry.** Combine grated pear, the grated half-onion, garlic, ginger, soy, rice wine, honey, sesame oil, and pepper in a bowl large enough for the beef. Stir until the honey dissolves — taste it: it should be sweet-salty-forward with pear perfume behind, assertive enough that you'd worry it's too strong. It isn't; the meat and the grill will mute it. Checkpoint — the marinade coats the back of a spoon and smells of pear and soy, with no raw garlic harshness on the nose (the ginger covers it).
  2. **Marry.** Add the beef and the sliced onion rings; toss with your hands (the Korean word for this hand-mix, jumulleok, is its own record #M06) until every shaving is coated. Press flat, cover, refrigerate 2–8 hours. Checkpoint — at grilling time the beef has turned deeper red-brown at the edges and the onion has gone slack. No pooling liquid at the bottom beyond the marinade itself — pooling means the pear was over-squeezed or the slice too thick.
  3. **Grill hot and fast, in batches.** Charcoal or a ripping cast-iron pan (a domed Korean bulgogi pan if you have one, 200 °C-plus at the surface — water should dance and vanish). Spread a single loose layer — piled meat steams in its own juice, and this dish's whole identity is browning plus juice, not steam. The shavings cook in 60–90 seconds a side. **Flip when the edges weep and color**, not on a clock: beads of pink juice at the rim and the first mahogany edge mean the grill boss turns the batch, then pulls it 20 seconds later when the raw red is just gone. Rest each batch on a warm plate; the moat juice goes to a small pot or the pan's rim to simmer with the mushrooms and carrot. Checkpoint — the eating test: a shaving straight off the grill should be caramel-sweet at the edge, juicy and soft in the middle, with no chewy gray band. Into a lettuce ssam with ssamjang (#SA01) it should taste like the genre's ceiling — but note honestly that the ssam is borrowed ceremony here; this dish's native table is rice, juice over rice, kimchi beside.
  4. **Serve the rice-thief properly.** The accumulated moat juice, simmered 2 minutes with any vegetables and poured over rice, is half the point of the lane. Don't discard it into the grill. Checkpoint — the bowl: rice, a ladle of juice, meat over, sesame and scallion. The juice should be sweet-savory and just glossy, not syrup; if it ran syrup, your pan was too cool and the batch steamed.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCauseFix
Meat gray and steamed, not brownedPan too cool or batch too bigFewer shavings per batch; wait for the pan to reheat between batches
Edges black, center rawSugar too high or heat too closeDrop honey a third; raise the grate; thinner slice
Meat mushy after marinatingPear too long at workCap at 8 hours; squeeze the grated pear drier next time
No juice for the riceCooked on a flat grate, all drippings lostCatch drippings in a pan beneath, or finish a batch domed-pan style

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated raw beef keeps 24 hours refrigerated (the clock is already running). Cooked bulgogi keeps 3 days and makes a superb kimchi fried rice or onigirazu filling; reheat hard and fast, never a slow simmer. - Variations: mushroom-soy umami build (#M02); the dry heritage fork (#M08); the gochujang lane is a different dish — spicy dwaeji bulgogi (#M13) — and pork, not this record.
