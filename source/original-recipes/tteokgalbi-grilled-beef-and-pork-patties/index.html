Tteokgalbi Grilled Beef-and-Pork Patties

Korean · Medium · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the ground road, honestly flagged.** Tteokgalbi is what happened when Korean cooks took the galbi flavor set and put it through the grinder: beef and pork minced fine, kneaded with the pear-soy marinade into a paste, and shaped around a stripped rib bone or into a thick oval patty — the name means "rice-cake galbi," for the patty's soft, chewy, almost tteok-like bite. Its own marketing often calls it court food; the honest version is that the court claim is contested, and what the documents actually show is the *Siuijeonseo* era's ground-meat skewer road leading toward it, with the dish's named regional identity arriving in the twentieth century. None of which matters at the grill: shaped on the bone or off it, lacquered and pressed, tteokgalbi is the juiciest thing this side of the bulgogi (#M01) lane and the most forgiving record on the meat list. Who cooks what: the patties are shaped ahead; the grill boss presses and flips; the table's youngest hands pull the bones out of the finished pieces — around a bare rib bone there is always a bone.

- **Knead it like dough — that is the technique.** Combine both meats with the squeezed pear, grated onion, garlic, and the liquid seasonings in a big bowl, and knead with your hands 5 full minutes, until the mix turns sticky and paste-like. The stickiness is myosin working; it is what lets a ground patty hold together on a grill grate without a casing of breading or egg. Under-kneaded mix falls apart at the flip — there is no fix at the grill, only the bowl. - **Rest the mix 2 hours, refrigerated.** The flavors marry and the pear's enzyme work relaxes everything. Overnight is acceptable; beyond that the texture goes from tender to mushy. - **Shape cold, thick, and oval.** With wet hands, divide into 8 portions (about 110 g each) and shape into thick ovals, 2 cm at the center. If you are using rib bones, press each portion firmly around the stripped bone lengthwise so the meat grips it. - **While the grill heats:** whisk the glaze in a small bowl and set it at the grill boss's station with a brush.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The mix: kneaded to sticky, 5 minutes; rested 2 hours; coarse grind; 110 g ovals, 2 cm thick.
  • The grill: medium fire, first side releases clean at 5 minutes, one deliberate press, no re-pressing.
  • The doneness: no pink in the center of a test patty — ground meat's rule — with juice still running at the cut.
  • The glaze: final 2 minutes, both faces, glossy and tacky, never candied or black.

Finish & Plate

Scissors-cut or whole ovals on a warm platter, glaze side up; the bone trough filled with ssamjang (#SA01) at the table if you went bone-in. Rice, ssam leaves, kkakdugi (#B02) and pickled onion for the acid cuts, and a stacked-bone plate for the ledger.

Tteokgalbi Grilled Beef-and-Pork Patties

Prep: 30 minutes, plus 2 hours resting · Cook: 16 minutes · Total: About 1 hour at the table (rest the mix ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The patties

  • 500 g beef chuck or short rib meat, ground coarse
  • 300 g pork shoulder, ground coarse (the beef-pork split is the classic juiciness build)
  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 150 g), grated and squeezed to a dry pulp
  • 1 small onion (about 100 g), grated
  • 5 cloves garlic (about 20 g), grated
  • 60 ml soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp honey or brown sugar (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • 4 strips of bone or sturdy metal skewers (optional; see Method step 3)

The glaze

  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml), 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g), 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml), 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g, optional)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), kimchi (#B01), pickled raw onion or kkakdugi (#B02)

Instructions

  1. **Grill over medium, not rocket-hot.** Charcoal at gray-edge with the grate a hand higher than for bulgogi shavings, or a pan at a solid medium (surface around 190 °C). The patty is thick and sweetened; a rocket fire blackens the glaze before the center cooks. Lay the patties with a finger's width between them, bone-end down if boned. Checkpoint — the first side is undisturbed for 5 minutes: the underside should be browned and release cleanly when lifted. A patty that sticks is a patty that isn't ready — the grate tells you when to flip, not the clock.
  2. **Flip once, then press.** Turn the patties, and press each one flat with a spatula — 30 percent thinner, a deliberate smash. The press is tteokgalbi's signature move: it widens the patty onto the grate for a second seared face, squeezes the rendered fat into the fire for the smoke that flavors the batch, and sets the dense-chewy texture the name promises. Press once; re-pressing squeezes out juice. Checkpoint — the pressed face browns 4 minutes; the patty is done at 8 minutes a side total when the center is just past pink at a test cut (ground meat cooks to no-pink, full stop — this is the doneness rule for every ground record on this site).
  3. **Glaze in the final 2 minutes, both faces.** Brush the glaze on the top face, let it set 60 seconds, flip, brush the second face, 60 seconds more, off the fire. The glaze should arrive glossy and just tacky — lacquered, not candied. If it runs and burns, the fire is still too hot for the glaze stage; finish the batch higher above the coals. Checkpoint — the finished patty: glossy dark glaze, two seared faces, dense and springy to the press of a finger, and — cut one at the grill station — juicy inside with no pink, the pear's sweetness woven through the meat rather than sitting on it.
  4. **Pull the bones at the table — the ceremony.** For bone-in patties, the bone slides free with a twist and a pull, leaving a clean trough; the bones stack on a side plate and the trough fills with a little ssamjang (#SA01) from the table's bowl. That is the old restaurant move, and children of every age take the bone duty seriously. Checkpoint — eating test, in a ssam: leaf, ssamjang swipe, a chunk of patty torn or scissors-cut from the oval, fold, one bite. The texture should be the record's signature — softer and chewier than any burger, juicier than bulgogi, the glaze's edge against the ssamjang's depth. If the center is dense and dry, the press was repeated or the mix overworked at shaping; if it falls apart, the knead was short.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The mix holds 24 hours refrigerated, shaped or loose; cooked patties keep 3 days and reheat hard and fast in a pan, glaze and all, or break into a lunchbox with rice and a fried egg. Freezer: 1 month, shaped and parchment-separated. - Variations: a strip of perilla leaf folded into each patty's center; the gochujang-glaze fork (a spoon of #M13's paste into the glaze); the unground fork is a different record entirely — neobiani (#M08) — and the two are not interchangeable, whatever the shared marketing says.
