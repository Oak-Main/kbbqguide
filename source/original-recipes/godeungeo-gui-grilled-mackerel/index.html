Godeungeo Gui Grilled Mackerel

Korean · Medium · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the whole-fish classic.** Godeungeo gui — salted mackerel, grilled whole or in cross-cut steaks until the skin crisps and the fat renders — is the fish record of the Korean home table: cheap, abundant, rich enough to feed a family on one fish, and forgiving enough to cook in a toaster oven, a broiler, a grill basket, or over charcoal. It is also the site's master class in whole-fish grilling: the salt cure, the drying skin, the single flip, and the doneness read at the backbone. Master this fish and every record around it (#SF08, #SF09) is a variation.

- **Salt early, salt more than instinct says.** Salt both fish's cavity and skin — 2 teaspoons feels like a lot, and it is the correct amount for this fat level: the cure seasons the flesh, firms it for the grill, and draws surface moisture to the skin where it will dry. A mackerel under-salted is the record's only unrecoverable failure; you can always add at the table, but you cannot cure after the fire. - **Rest salted, refrigerated and uncovered, 30 minutes to 2 hours** (longer is better — the classic home method salts the fish in the morning and grills at dinner). Before cooking, pat the skin bone-dry: wet skin steams, dry skin crisps. - **Score the thickest part** — three diagonal cuts to the bone on each side of whole fish. The scores are doneness windows (you watch the flesh at the bone) and they keep the skin from splitting as it shrinks. - **Fire for moderate, indirect-adjacent:** charcoal at medium with a cooler zone (roughly 180–200 °C over the cooking side — a hand held at the grate counts 3–4 seconds before flinching), or a broiler/grill basket arrangement where flare-ups can be dodged. Mackerel's fat drips and catches; the plan is to manage that, not prevent it.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The fish: cured with the full salt measure, dried uncovered, skin patted bone-dry, thickest part scored to the bone.
  • The fire: moderate with a cool zone; the skin releases itself — never forced.
  • The pull: flesh opaque at the backbone at the thickest score, pulling from the bone with a nudge.
  • The plate: two fillets off one skeleton, skin crisp, fermented cut beside every plate.

Finish & Plate

Warm platter, the skeleton lifted clear at the table (children and grandfathers compete for the honor), sesame and scallion over the fillets. Rice, kimchi, mu sangchae, the onion-soy dip (#SA05) within reach, and the lemon wedge for whoever wants the acid brighter.

Godeungeo Gui Grilled Mackerel

Prep: 20 minutes, plus 30 minutes salting · Cook: 12 minutes · Total: About 1 hour 5 minutes · Serves 4

Ingredients

The fish

  • 2 whole mackerel (about 350 g each), gutted and cleaned, heads on or off (the Korean market's default is dressed; both are correct), or 4 thick cross-cut steaks (about 450 g)

The cure

  • 2 tsp coarse salt (about 10 g) — more than seems right; see Method
  • Freshly ground black pepper

The grill and the finish

  • Neutral oil for the grate (about 10 ml)
  • 1 lemon or a splash of rice vinegar (about 15 ml), optional but traditional beside
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g) and sliced scallion (about 10 g), for the platter

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), kimchi (#B01) — the fatty-fish law is a fermented cut beside it — mu sangchae (about 100 g), and a bowl of the soy-vinegar onion dip (#SA05)

Instructions

  1. **Oiled grate, skin down, over the moderate zone — and leave it alone.** Whole fish need 5–6 minutes on the first side; steaks 3–4. The skin will stick and then release itself as it crisps — the earliest tongs attempt tears the skin off and the skin is half the dish. Checkpoint — the skin is done when it lifts cleanly and shows crisp gold-brown with darker grill stripes; at the thickest score, the flesh below is opaque halfway to the bone. Fat will drip and flare — move the fish to the cool zone for 20 seconds when it does, back to finish. This dance is the whole technique.
  2. **The one flip, gently.** Two spatulas or one wide fish spatula and a confident turn: flesh side (or second steak face) down for 4–5 minutes on the moderate zone. The second side takes less time than the first — the fish is half-cooked already. Checkpoint — the doneness read at the backbone, the record's core test: at the thickest score on the second side, the flesh at the bone has just turned opaque and pulls away from the bone with a fork's gentle sideways nudge. Opaque-at-the-bone is done; translucent-at-the-bone is 2 more minutes; falling-apart is past. Mackerel is fatty enough to stay moist a full minute past done — use that grace, do not fear it, but do not bank on it either.
  3. **The plate is the filet lesson.** Whole fish to a warm platter; the top fillet lifts off the bones in one piece with a spatula run along the backbone (peel from head-end to tail). The skeleton lifts clear in one motion, the second fillet lies beneath — the oldest two-fillets-one-fish service in the world. Sesame and scallion over, lemon or vinegar beside. Checkpoint — eating test: a flake of the thick loin with a strand of kimchi — crisp skin in the same bite, rich sweet fat, the fermented cut answering it. If the flesh tastes under-seasoned, the cure ran shy (fix next fish: the full 2 teaspoons); the vinegar splash at the table is the patch, not the cure.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The salted fish holds 24 hours refrigerated (the cure is the keeping). Cooked, 2 days — the leftover fillet flaked over rice with sesame oil and a fried egg is the home table's day-after classic, arguably better than the first night. - Variations: hairtail runs the same cure sliced into fingers (#SF08); yellow croaker runs it whole and drier-salted (#SF09); a gochujang swipe in the last 2 minutes takes it toward the yangnyeom lane for the spicy table.
