**Stream: contemporary — the Korean answer to the miso cod.** The famous miso-marinated black cod and this record share a fish and a technique family: a fermented bean paste cure, a long rest, and a slow sweet glaze under heat. The Korean version runs doenjang where the Japanese-American restaurant version runs miso — deeper, funkier, a shade less sweet — and the result is the same miracle from the other direction: buttery fish under a caramelized lacquer. Labeled honestly: this is a contemporary build, the pantry's oldest paste on a premium fish, and it is the site's special-occasion seafood record.

- **The cure, 24–48 hours ahead, is the recipe.** Whisk the cure's ingredients smooth and paint the fillets thickly on all faces; stack them in a dish, cover, refrigerate, turning once at the halfway mark. The paste's salt and enzymes work slowly into the flesh — at 24 hours the fish is seasoned; at 48 it is transformed, firm-silky and deeply savory. This is the one record on the site where "marinate longer" is always the right answer, and it is why the day-of work is fifteen minutes. - **Decide the skin question before cooking:** skin-on gives the crisp base and the fat's protection but shields the cure's contact; skin-off (this record's default for the full lacquer experience) wants a gentle touch and a moderate oven-or-grill path. Choose lacquer over crisp, or crisp over lacquer — both are correct, name the choice at the table. - **Rinse or not:** scrape the excess cure off the surface (leave the thin film that clings), but do not rinse the fillet — the rinse takes the cure's first two millimeters with it. - **Fire for moderate and indirect-leaning:** charcoal at medium with the fish offset, or a 200 °C oven finished under a hot broiler. The sugars caramelize over ten minutes; direct high heat takes them past caramel in two.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The cure: 24–48 hours, turned once, excess scraped not rinsed.
  • The fire: moderate and offset; tea-brown caramel, never black.
  • The pull: parts at a fork's weight, translucent-glossy center — custard-soft, fully safe at the flake stage.
  • The plate: sharp pickle beside every serving; the richness is the foil, not the point.

Finish & Plate

One fillet per person on warm rice or a wide spoon, sesame and scallion over the lacquered face, the pickle plate passing. This is the seafood record that behaves like a main course — set it at the table's center and let the lacquer make the argument.

Doenjang-Glazed Grilled Black Cod

Prep: 20 minutes, plus 24–48 hours curing · Cook: 12 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes on the day (cure ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The fish

  • 4 black cod (sablefish) fillets (about 700 g), skin on or off (see Method), pin bones pulled

The cure (24–48 hours ahead)

  • 3 tbsp doenjang (about 60 g)
  • 2 tbsp mirin (30 ml)
  • 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 tsp ginger (about 2 g), grated

The finishing glaze

  • 2 tbsp honey (about 40 g), 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml), 1 tsp soy sauce (5 ml)

The finish and table

  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g), thin-sliced scallion (about 10 g)
  • Steamed rice (about 500 g cooked), pickled perilla (#B24) or quick-pickled cucumber, and a plain lettuce salad — the rich lacquer wants sharp, simple things

Instructions

  1. **Slow face first:** skin-on goes skin-down, skin-off goes presentation-face-down, on the moderate zone. Six to eight minutes — the surface caramelizes slowly from paste-pale to tea-brown, and the fillet's underside sets. Checkpoint — the surface color reads deep tea with darker edges; the flesh at the sides has turned opaque a third of the way up. No black — black means the zone is running hot; offset further from the coals.
  2. **Flip with a full spatula's support — black cod is soft as custard when raw and nearly as soft when cooked.** Brush the caramelized first face with the finishing glaze, two minutes on the second side, glaze the second face, and pull at the flake stage: the flesh at the thickest point parts along the grain with a fork's weight and no force, still translucent-glossy at the very center (black cod's fat keeps it silky well past where other fish dry — around 55 °C internal at the center if you measure). Checkpoint — the test flake: sweet-savory lacquer outside, butter-fat silk inside, the doenjang's depth all the way through rather than sitting on the surface — that all-the-way-through is the 48-hour cure's signature, and no glaze trick can fake it.
  3. **Sesame and scallion over, and serve in wide spoons or on rice — the fillet barely holds a shape.** The sharp pickle beside every plate; the lacquer's sweetness and the pickle's acid are the plate's argument. Checkpoint — eating test: the first bite should be lacquer, silk, then the pickle's cut — the three-beat finish. If the pickle is an afterthought on the plate it will be an afterthought on the palate; this fish makes its own case for why Korean tables always set a sharp thing beside the rich one.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The cure is the keeping: curing fillets hold 48 hours (the clock above), cooked fish 2 days — cold flakes over rice with the thinned glaze is the day-after luxury. - Variations: the gochujang-glaze fork on the same cure clock (#SF10); the cure's logic on thick salmon at 12 hours; the scraped-off excess cure, simmered with a splash of water and strained, is a spoon-sauce for the platter — the zero-waste finish.
