**Stream: heritage — the purist's bowl.** Gireumjang is three ingredients and a warning that it looks like nothing: toasted sesame oil, coarse salt, and black pepper. It is the dip that the unmarinated records — the classic samgyeopsal (#M10), the galbisal (#M05), the neobiani (#M08) — actually want, because it seasons without competing: the meat tastes like meat, the oil carries the salt, the pepper warms the finish. The neobiani record calls it the period-honest companion for exactly this reason. Five minutes, and the table's quietest bowl is its most important.

Gireumjang Sesame-Oil Salt Dip

Prep: 5 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 5 minutes · Makes about 80 ml (a table's bowl)

Ingredients

The dip

  • 4 tbsp toasted sesame oil (60 ml)
  • 1 tsp coarse salt (about 5 g) — flaky or kosher, not fine table salt
  • 1/2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper (about 2 g)

The optional build-outs (tradition allows either, never both loudly)

  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), finely sliced, folded in
  • 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g), lightly crushed

Instructions

  1. Oil in the bowl, salt and pepper over, a stir — the salt suspends, swirls, settles. That is the dish. Checkpoint — the visual: visible salt grains suspended in amber oil, pepper flecking it. Taste on a piece of the plainest grilled pork: a spark of salt, the oil's roast-nut roundness, pepper's warmth — and the meat louder for it, not quieter. That inversion is the bowl's whole argument.
  2. Serve in a shallow bowl (deep oil is a dunk hazard — a shallow pool is a touch-dip) beside the unmarinated meats, and say nothing; the table finds it. Checkpoint — by the second round of samgyeopsal, the ssamjang bowl (#SA01) and this bowl are both half-empty and the table has chosen its camps — or splits by mood, which is the usual result and the reason both bowls are set.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 3 days refrigerated (the salt stays suspended with a stir; the oil solidifies faintly cold and re-liquefies at room temperature in minutes). Make-the-day-of is the tradition. - Variations: the scallion build-out for the pork records; a pinch of gochugaru for the table that cannot have an unspiced bowl; the same ratio on butter for the seafood records' (#SF12's) lane — a fusion fork, labeled as one.
