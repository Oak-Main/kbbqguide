LA-Style Galbi

Korean-American · Medium · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: diaspora.** LA galbi is the Korean-American chapter of the galbi story: beef short ribs cut flanken-style, across the bone, into thin strips about a centimeter thick — a cut immigrant butchers in 1970s and 80s Southern California turned into the backyard and restaurant grill's finest hour. The name honors where the cut became standard, not where the dish was invented; the soy-pear-sugar marinade it wears is the sweet register of the postwar era, dialled further for American palates. Cooked right it is one of the best things that ever happened to a charcoal grill: lacquered edges, juicy meat, and a strip of bone the table fights over. This record is the honest diaspora master — sweeter than the Seoul classic, unmistakably galbi. Who cooks what: one grill boss with tongs and scissors, a designated bone-counter keeping the pieces fair, everyone else on ssam duty.

- **Blend the marinade.** Unlike bulgogi, where the aromatics are grated for a slurry, galbi's marinade is better blitzed smooth in a blender — pear, onion, garlic, ginger with the soy, sugar, mirin, sesame oil, and pepper. Thirty seconds, completely smooth. The sugar dissolves fully and every millimeter of the flanken strip takes the bath evenly. - **Trim and score.** Look at each strip: pull off the membrane on the bone side if the butcher left it, and score the meat between the bones in a shallow crosshatch — two or three light cuts each way. The scores let the marinade reach the center and stop the strips from cupping on the grill. - **Marinate 6 hours minimum, 24 maximum**, refrigerated, in a flat dish so the strips lie in one layer. Flip once halfway. - Pull the ribs out 30 minutes before grilling; cold galbi on hot charcoal steams gray at the bone before the surface lacquers.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The cut: flanken, 8–10 mm, crosshatch scored, membrane off.
  • The marinade: blended smooth, 6–24 hours, flat single layer.
  • The grill: mahogany lacquer, char in the scores, no raw red at the bone, a sliver of pink allowed.
  • The table: scissors to single bones, ssam while hot, bones gnawed.

Finish & Plate

Warm platter at center, scissors left in the pile — the table cuts as it eats. Ssam leaves, ssamjang (#SA01), pajeori (#F02), sliced garlic, and rice within every reach. The gnawed bones stack on a side plate, and the count at the end is the meal's honest ledger.

LA-Style Galbi

Prep: 25 minutes, plus 6–24 hours marinating · Cook: 12 minutes · Total: About 40 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The ribs

  • 1.6 kg beef short ribs, flanken-cut across the bone, 8–10 mm thick (6–8 pieces; order "LA galbi cut" at a Korean market)

The marinade

  • 1 Asian pear (about 300 g), grated with juice
  • 1 onion (about 150 g), roughly chopped
  • 1 head garlic's worth, 8 cloves (about 32 g)
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 20 g)
  • 150 ml soy sauce
  • 80 g brown sugar or honey
  • 60 ml mirin or 40 ml soju
  • 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil (30 ml)
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g)
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g), sliced; 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce and perilla leaves for ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), pajeori (#F02), kimchi (#B01), garlic cloves sliced thick

Instructions

  1. **Set the fire for medium-hot, two-zone if you can.** Charcoal burned down to gray-edged coals, or a grill you can hold your hand over for 3–4 seconds at the cooking height (around 200 °C). Galbi's sugar wants to blacken; a cooler zone gives you an escape hatch when a strip lacquers before the meat at the bone is done. Lay the strips in one layer, bone side down first. Checkpoint — the first minute tells you if the fire is right: steady sizzle, light smoke, the smell of caramelizing soy. Violent flare-ups mean drip fat found the coals — move the strip, don't lose it.
  2. **Grill 3–4 minutes a side, flipping once or twice.** The grill boss's law: leave the strips alone until the edges weep and the underside goes mahogany, then flip with tongs — never a fork; the juice is the point. The second side takes a minute less. If a strip's sugars run ahead of the meat, shift it to the cool zone and close the lid for 90 seconds; steam finishes the center while the lacquer rests. Checkpoint — the pull test: the meat between the bones has firmed, the surface is glossy mahogany with char in the crosshatch scores, and the cut edge at a bone shows no raw red — a sliver of pink at the bone is right, raw is not. One test piece eaten at the grill boss's station is traditional and non-negotiable.
  3. **Scissors, platter, ssam.** Pull the strips and cut them with kitchen scissors into single-bone pieces, straight onto a warm platter at the table's center. Sesame and scallion over; the pieces go straight into waiting ssam: leaf, a thin swipe of ssamjang (#SA01), a piece of galbi while it is still hot, a strand of pajeori or a slice of raw garlic, fold, one bite. The bone gets gnawed at the table; that is what bones are for. Checkpoint — the ssam test: one bite, everything at once — sweet lacquer edge, juicy beef, ssamjang's fermented depth, the leaf's snap. If the galbi overpowers the ssamjang, the marinade ran sweet; trim the sugar next batch by a fifth. If the ssam falls apart, less is in the leaf than you think is enough.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated strips keep 24 hours (freeze in the marinade up to a month, thaw overnight — the pear keeps working, so freeze flat and early). Cooked galbi keeps 3 days; chop and fry into kimchi fried rice with the bones for stock. - Variations: the garlic-sesame boneless galbisal (#M05) is the same joint's quieter cousin; the gochujang lacquer fork (#M04) for the spice-first table; braised galbijjim is another record's road entirely — do not braise this cut into it.
