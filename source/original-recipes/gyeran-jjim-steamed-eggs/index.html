Gyeran Jjim Steamed Eggs

Korean · Easy · Serves 2–3 (one pot)

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the bubbling pot.** Gyeran jjim is the banchan that arrives still boiling: eggs whipped with water and a whisper of salt, steamed in a small pot or earthenware vessel until they rise into a golden, soufflé-soft cloud, finished with scallion and sesame oil and served sizzling. It is the grill table's hot custard — the dish the table shares spoon-first straight from the vessel, and the one that vanishes in ninety seconds flat. The craft is one ratio (egg to water) and one heat law (gentle, lidded, undisturbed).

Quantities live in the recipe card.

The eggs

The vessel

- **The ratio is the recipe: 4 eggs to 160 ml liquid.** More liquid and it steams to a tender soup-soft set (the pour-over-rice school); less and it goes dense and rubbery. Measure the first time; eyeball it forever after as "a little under one egg's volume of water per egg." - **Whip it thoroughly** — this is the opposite instruction from gyeran mari (#B11): 60 seconds of real whipping, incorporating air, because the steam needs that air to lift the eggs into the cloud. Strain through a fine sieve if you want the restaurant's flawlessness; skip it for the home honesty. - Set the steamer or the lidded pot arrangement, and start the table's rice — the eggs finish fast.

Gyeran Jjim Steamed Eggs

Prep: 5 minutes · Cook: 12 minutes · Total: About 17 minutes · Serves 2–3 (one pot)

Ingredients

The eggs

  • 4 large eggs (about 200 g), room temperature
  • 160 ml warm water (or light anchovy-kelp stock, the deeper traditional build)
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)
  • A drop of toasted sesame oil (about 2 ml), 1 scallion (about 15 g) sliced, 1 tsp sesame seeds (about 4 g), optional red chili thread

The vessel

  • A small heatproof pot, ceramic ttukbaegi, or ramekins (the traditional single-serve); a lid or foil

Instructions

  1. The egg mixture poured into the vessel, any additions (a single anchovy, a piece of fish cake) hidden at the bottom — the treasure custom. Lid on, vessel set over the lowest heat your stove holds (or into a steamer over gentle steam). Checkpoint — nothing should happen visibly for 4–5 minutes — slow heat is the cloud's whole architecture. A rapid boil makes gray-rubbery eggs in eight minutes; resist checking.
  2. Steam gently 10–12 minutes lidded, until the center has set to a soft tremble and the edges have pulled into a puffed, risen dome. Checkpoint — the eating test at the pot: a spoon's edge should sink in with the faintest resistance and come away carrying a clean, moist, golden curd — softer than any omelet, just short of pourable. If the spoon comes away with weeping liquid, 90 more seconds; if the curd feels bouncy, it crossed into rubber — lower heat, next time.
  3. Sesame oil, scallion, sesame, chili thread over, and the pot — still bubbling at the edges from its own heat — goes to the table on its trivet. Checkpoint — the shared-pot ritual: spoons in from every side while the surface still shivers, and the bowl empties before it stops steaming. That timing is the dish's true doneness.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- There is no storage — gyeran jjim exists for ninety seconds and then it is a memory. Raw whipped base holds 1 day refrigerated, covered. - Variations: cheese folded in mid-steam (the school-café fork); saeujeot or a hidden anchovy at the pot's floor; the individual ramekin service, one per diner, is the restaurant's (and the children's) preferred format.
