Korean Potato Salad

Korean · Easy · Serves 6–8 as banchan

**Stream: contemporary — the Western-contact bowl, claimed.** Korean potato salad is what happened when Western salad met the Korean banchan table: mashed potato bound lightly with mayo and fruit — apple, sometimes raisins or cucumber — sweet, soft, and completely unbothered by anyone's definition of salad. It arrived in the twentieth century, stayed for the children, and every Korean grill restaurant now serves it (#B22's macaroni sibling beside it). Labeled honestly as the modern bowl it is; loved without reservation, because it works.

Korean Potato Salad

Prep: 20 minutes · Cook: 20 minutes · Total: About 40 minutes · Serves 6–8 as banchan

Ingredients

The potatoes

  • 700 g russet or yukon gold potatoes, peeled, cut into 4 cm chunks

The bind and the mix-ins

  • 3 tbsp Kewpie-style Japanese mayonnaise (about 45 g) — the one non-negotiable brand-loyalty in this record
  • 1 tsp sugar (about 4 g)
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt (about 3 g)
  • 1/2 small apple (about 75 g), small-diced
  • 1/2 cucumber (about 75 g), small-diced and salted 10 minutes
  • 2 hard-boiled eggs (about 100 g), chopped
  • 1 scallion or 2 tbsp minced onion (about 10 g), optional
  • Black pepper

Instructions

  1. Rice the potatoes or mash them coarse — deliberately coarse, with visible small chunks left in. Korean potato salad is not a smooth puree; the texture target is "mostly mashed, part smashed," with bits that keep the bowl interesting. Checkpoint — the mash holds a spoon's trail briefly and no water pools at the bowl's edge (if it does, the dry-steam was skipped — work quickly now, the mayo comes later).
  2. Fold while just-warm — this is the timing law: the sugar, salt, and about two-thirds of the mayo folded into warm potato (it melts in and seasons evenly), then the apple, squeezed cucumber, eggs, and scallion folded in as it cools. Checkpoint — the fold test at the end: the mix-ins distribute without mashing further, and every spoonful carries apple and egg — it should look generous, not potato-with-flecks.
  3. Chill 30 minutes minimum — the bowl is served cold, and the flavors need the chill to marry. The remaining mayo over the top, a grind of pepper. Checkpoint — the eating test, cold: soft-sweet, the apple's crisp-sweet snap and the cucumber's cool, the egg's richness, mayo's tang behind everything — a children's champion with a grown-up following, and the perfect cooling counter at a heavy grill table.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2 days refrigerated (the cucumber's clock) — a picnic and lunchbox staple by tradition. - Variations: raisins or canned fruit cocktail folded in (the old-school café build, sweeter than modern taste but the grandmothers' original); the macaroni sibling (#B22); a layer of the salad inside a gimbap roll — its most beloved deployment.
