Gochujang Samgyeopsal

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

**Stream: restaurant, the dressed lane.** Take the classic salt-and-sesame samgyeopsal (#M10), paint the belly with a gochujang paste before the fire, and the dish changes personality: the render still does the cooking, but now there is a lacquer on the crisp edge and a slow ferment-heat under the fat. The dressed belly is a fixture of every tabletop restaurant that discovered its regulars wanted the classic's texture with more on top. It asks one thing the classic does not — paste discipline. Too much and you are grilling sauce, not pork.

Gochujang Samgyeopsal

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 30 minutes resting · Cook: 20 minutes at the table · Total: About 35 minutes at the table · Serves 4

Ingredients

The pork

  • 1.2 kg pork belly, skin off, cut into slabs 1 cm thick (slightly thinner than the classic — the paste wants faster cooking)

The paste

  • 3 tbsp gochujang (about 60 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g), for the heat-forward table

The table

  • Lettuce and perilla ssam, ssamjang (#SA01) thinned with a splash of water for dipping, kimchi (#B01), steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), garlic coins and green chili slices

Instructions

  1. Slabs on the pan in a single layer, medium-low. The first minutes are quiet rendering again — the paste darkens one shade at the edges and the fat begins to gloss the pan. Turn every 90 seconds. Checkpoint — the paste should deepen toward brick and smell toasted-chile-sweet, not burnt. Any black edges on the first turn mean the flame is a notch high — lower it and the batch is fine.
  2. At 12–14 minutes, scissors into bite pieces directly on the pan, toss in the hot fat 60 seconds for the crisp lacquered faces, and pull to the platter. Checkpoint — test piece: crisp lacquered edge, the three-layer belly interior, then the paste's ferment-heat arriving after the fat. If the heat lands before the pork, the film was thick — thinner next batch.
  3. Ssam sequence as the classic: leaf, thin ssamjang swipe, hot pork piece, garlic or chili, fold, one bite. Rice and kimchi between. Checkpoint — the ssam test should read as the classic's rich cousin — same crisp, same fat, a red edge on the flavor — not as a sweet-spicy pork candy. When in doubt, thin the paste further; the pork is the headline.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Painted slabs hold 24 hours refrigerated (the curing clock is running). Cooked, 3 days; dice into fried rice or fold into a lunchbox gimbap-style roll. - Variations: the undressed classic (#M10) is the parent; a spoon of the paste into the ssamjang dip instead of onto the meat splits the difference; kimchi beside the slabs on the pan, same as ever.
