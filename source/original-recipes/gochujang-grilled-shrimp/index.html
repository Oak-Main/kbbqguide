Gochujang Grilled Shrimp

Korean · Easy · Serves 3–4

**Stream: contemporary — the yangnyeom lane on shellfish.** Shrimp are the fastest protein at any grill, and the gochujang glaze is the loudest flavor on this site's seafood list; together they make the record that converts skeptics at a mixed grill. The law is one sentence: glaze at the end, never at the start. Shrimp cook in three minutes and the paste burns in two — the glaze goes on when the shrimp are already almost done.

Gochujang Grilled Shrimp

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 30 minutes marinating · Cook: 6 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes · Serves 3–4

Ingredients

The shrimp

  • 700 g large shrimp (16–20 count), shell on, deveined, or shell-off if the table prefers peeling-free eating

The glaze

  • 2 tbsp gochujang (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)
  • 2 cloves garlic (about 8 g), grated
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g), optional heat dial

The finish and table

  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g), sliced scallion (about 10 g)
  • Steamed rice (about 400 g cooked), oi muchim (#B07), lettuce ssam

Instructions

  1. Dry shrimp onto a hot grate or pan in a single layer, two minutes undisturbed. If shell-on, they will char lightly at the edges; that char is the flavor and the table peels it off later. Checkpoint — the shells blush pink-white and the undersides show grill marks through the shell; shell-off shrimp turn opaque at the bottom edge and roll loosely on the grate.
  2. Flip once, one minute more — the shrimp are nearly done now. Brush the glaze on the up faces, wait 30 seconds, flip, brush the second faces, 30 seconds, off the fire. Checkpoint — the glaze sets glossy and brick-toned in that final minute. If it blackens, the fire was past the glaze's tolerance — the shrimp are still good; note the notch down for the next batch.
  3. Sesame and scallion over, platter, straight to the table — shrimp wait for nobody. Checkpoint — eating test: sweet-heat glaze on the first chew, then the shrimp's own snap and sweetness — the glaze should coat, not bury. One test shrimp that tastes only of gochujang means the coating ran thick; a thin brush-stroke is the whole technique.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Raw shrimp in the glaze hold 30 minutes, no more — the salt starts curing. Cooked, 2 days; the shells make a quick stock for the next day's jjigae. - Variations: the soy-garlic fork (#SF02) for the clean lane; the same glaze on scallops (#SF03's cousin); cho-gochujang (#SA03) served beside as the dip instead of brushed.
