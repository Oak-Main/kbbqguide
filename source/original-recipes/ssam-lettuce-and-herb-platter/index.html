**Stream: heritage — the table's other main dish.** The ssam platter is not a side. On this site's grammar it is half the architecture of the meal: a basket of leaves and herbs, washed, dried, and built for wrapping, sitting beside the meat with the ssamjang (#SA01) and the dips. Every grilled-meat record from the bulgogi (#M01) to the lamb (#M20) points at this platter, and the meal's best bites happen inside it. This record is the assembly: what leaves, what each one does, and how the table builds the wrap.

- **Wash everything, dry everything completely.** Wet leaves tear at the fold and water down the ssamjang into a slick; a salad spinner and a towel hour is the whole prep. Cold water, then the fridge until serving — the chill is part of the contrast with hot meat off the fire. - Sort by leaf: the big lettuce cups stacked flat, the perilla in its own pile (it bruises under weight), the herbs in water and spun at the last minute. - Slice the garlic thick and soak the onion — the ten-minute cold soak takes its raw bite to the sweet-edged place the wrap wants.

Ssam Lettuce and Herb Platter

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes · Serves 4 (the table's foundation)

Ingredients

The leaves (the wrap chassis — a generous double-handful of leaves per person)

  • 1 head green leaf lettuce (about 300 g) — the everyday wrap, mild cup
  • 1 head red leaf or butter lettuce (about 250 g) — the tender alternative
  • 20–30 perilla leaves (kkaennip, about 40 g) — the flavor wrap, anise-mint
  • 1 bunch napa cabbage leaves, the tender inner ones (about 200 g), optional — the crisp snap wrap

The herbs and crunch (threaded into the wraps)

  • 1 small bunch watercress or minari (about 50 g)
  • Garlic cloves, sliced into thick coins (about 40 g), raw
  • Green chili or jalapeño, sliced (about 20 g)
  • Thinly sliced onion, soaked 10 minutes in cold water (about 100 g)

The table's other half (beside the platter, from this site)

  • Ssamjang (#SA01), gireumjang (#SA02), the scallion-soy dip (#SA04), a bowl of rice, and the banchan board

Instructions

  1. **The platter's arrangement — bigger leaves under, herbs through.** The lettuce cups fanned around the platter's rim as the chassis layer, perilla and cabbage tucked inside the ring, garlic coins, chili, and drained onion in small piles at the center where every hand reaches. Checkpoint — the geometry test: every diner, from any seat, can reach a cup, a perilla leaf, and a center pile without asking anyone to pass. The platter turns; build it round.
  2. **The wrap build, the table's own method.** The sequence this site's M-records keep citing: one leaf (or a lettuce cup lined with one perilla leaf — the double-bottom, the connoisseur's build), a thin swipe of ssamjang or a finger-dip of gireumjang, the meat while it is still hot, a garlic coin or chili slice or onion thread, a pinch of rice if the wrap runs meat-only, fold stem-side over, tuck the sides, one bite. Checkpoint — the fold closes without tearing and delivers everything in a single bite: that is the doneness of a ssam. Two bites means the leaf was overfilled; a torn wrap means the sauce ran to the fold — swipe thinner, higher.
  3. **The pacing — the platter sets the meal's rhythm.** Meat, wrap, rice, banchan, repeat. The leaves are the meal's brakes and its carriers; a table that runs out of leaves starts eating meat with chopsticks alone, and the meal quietly becomes a different, heavier thing. Checkpoint — the end-of-meal test: empty leaf platter, empty ssamjang bowl, and the grill's last pieces going into the last wraps — the platter planned to the end is the record's real success metric.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Washed leaves keep 2–3 days in paper towel in a bag in the fridge — the make-ahead that makes a weeknight grill possible. Herbs, 2 days in water. - Variations: steamed cabbage leaves and blanched kale for winter's sturdy wraps; thin rice-paper wrappers at the table's edge for the fusion lane; the pickled perilla (#B24) as a wrap-within-a-wrap, the double-perilla build.
