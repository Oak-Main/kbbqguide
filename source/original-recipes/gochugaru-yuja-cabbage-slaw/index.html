**Stream: contemporary — the citrus build.** This slaw is the modern Korean table's answer to coleslaw: napa cabbage in a gochugaru dressing brightened with yuja (Korean citron — marmalade's source) instead of vinegar alone. It carries the geotjeori family's (#B04, #F03) fresh-kimchi logic into a rounder, citrus-forward register, and it is the fresh bowl that best matches the grilled fish records (#SF07, #SF09), whose oiliness wants citrus the way the meat records want scallion (#F02).

Gochugaru-Yuja Cabbage Slaw

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes, best within 2 hours · Serves 4–6 as the table's salad

Ingredients

The cabbage

  • 400 g napa cabbage, the crisp stems section, cut into ribbons 1 cm wide
  • 1/2 tsp coarse salt (about 3 g), for the 10-minute draw

The dressing

  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (about 6 g)
  • 1 tbsp yuja-cheong (citron marmalade, about 25 g) — or 1 tbsp honey plus the zest and juice of half a lemon
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp soy sauce (5 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced; a pinch of black pepper

Instructions

  1. The dressing whisked smooth — gochugaru, yuja, vinegar, soy — into an amber-red slurry that tastes citrus-first, savory-second, with the gochugaru's warmth behind. Checkpoint — a fingertip taste should read like marmalade that went to the grill table: bright, sweet, a warm hum. Sharper or sweeter is a teaspoon of vinegar or yuja away.
  2. The squeezed cabbage, scallion, and the dressing tossed — spoons this time, gently, the ribbons are sturdy but the toss is short — sesame oil and seeds through. Checkpoint — the eating test: the stem's snap and the citrus dressing's bright-sweet-heat in one bite, with the yuja rind's bitter-edge flick at the finish — the note that separates this slaw from every honey-and-chili imitation. Beside grilled mackerel (#SF07), the fish's fat and the citrus meet exactly as intended.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Best within 2 hours, edible at a day refrigerated (the ribbons hold better than lettuce); undressed salted cabbage keeps half a day. - Variations: matchstick apple folded in for sweetness and snap; the same dressing on cucumber ribbons; a pinch of gochugaru more for the tables that want the slaw to carry heat as well as brightness.
