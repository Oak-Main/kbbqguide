Classic Samgyeopsal with Sesame-Salt Seasoning

Korean · Easy · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: restaurant — the pork commons.** Samgyeopsal is the people's steak: thick slabs of pork belly, unmarinated, seasoned with nothing at the start but salt and sesame, grilled in the middle of the table until the fat renders and the edges crisp, then folded into lettuce with ssamjang, raw garlic, and green chili. The dish as a national institution is young — dedicated shops cluster in the late 1970s, and portable butane burners in the 1980s took the grill into every walk-up apartment — but the logic is old: a shared pan in the middle of the table, cooked by whoever's turn it is, so labor and talk share the fire. This record is the classic salt-and-sesame build. The gochujang-dressed cousin (#M11) is one lane over. Who cooks what: the tongs rotate around the table by convention, one flip per person. The grill boss of the evening only manages the fire itself.

- **Nothing marinates. That is the recipe's first instruction.** The pork belly's job is to taste like pork and fire; every other flavor arrives at the ssam stage. Salt lightly just before the meat hits the grate, not before — salted-ahead belly weeps, and weeping meat steams. - **Set the full table before the fire starts.** Once the pork is grilling, nobody leaves their seat: both dips made, garlic and chili sliced, lettuce spun bone-dry, rice hot. The dips sit on the table's turntable with the meat platter. - **Fire for steady medium, not searing.** A tabletop gas burner on medium under a ridged pan or dome (a pan surface in the 180 °C range — water skitters, oil shimmer-strips), or charcoal at a gentle hand-count of 5 seconds. Pork belly renders its own fat for the cooking; the fire's only job is patience. Have a folded paper towel and tongs ready to manage flare-ups.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The slabs: 1.5 cm, unmarinated, salted only at the second turn.
  • The render: translucent-gold edges, steady sputter, no hard smoke.
  • The scissors: cut at deep golden, tossed 60–90 seconds for crisp faces.
  • The bite: three layers — crisp, yielding fat, tender meat — in one closed ssam.

Finish & Plate

The pan itself is the platter, refilled slab by slab at the table's center on its trivet. Both dip bowls flanking, the ssam leaves in a basket, grilled garlic and scallions on the side plate, rice and kimchi in reach. The bones-free ledger here is a plate of empties: how many ssam, how many bites — the count is the conversation.

Classic Samgyeopsal with Sesame-Salt Seasoning

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 25 minutes at the table · Total: About 40 minutes at the table · Serves 4

Ingredients

The pork

  • 1.2 kg fresh pork belly, skin off, cut into slabs 1.5 cm thick and 5 cm wide (order "samgyeopsal cut" at a Korean market; the even ribbon shape is part of the dish)

The only seasoning for the grill

  • Coarse sea salt (about 6 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g), lightly crushed
  • Freshly ground black pepper

The dips (both belong on the table)

  • Gireumjang (#SA02): 3 tbsp toasted sesame oil (45 ml), 1 tsp coarse salt (about 5 g), a grind of pepper — the classic samgyeopsal dip
  • Ssamjang (#SA01): about 120 g from its record

The ssam and cuts

  • 1 head lettuce and 20 perilla leaves, washed and bone-dry
  • 8 cloves garlic (about 32 g), sliced thick coins
  • 3 green chilies (about 30 g), sliced diagonally
  • 1 bundle scallions or 100 g onion, thick-cut rings (the traditional grate-mates, grilled beside the pork in its fat)
  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), kimchi (#B01), and a bottle of soju's worth of ceremony

Instructions

  1. **Lay the slabs flat, one layer, at the pan's center.** They will not brown in the first minutes — they will render. Turn them every 2 minutes as the fat liquefies and the pan develops a shallow gloss of it. Salt faces lightly on the second turn; sesame seeds and pepper on the third. Checkpoint — the sound changes from a hiss to a steady sputter and the edges go translucent-gold: rendering is doing the cooking. If the pan smokes hard, lower the flame — burnt pork fat is bitter and it flavors the whole batch.
  2. **Grill the grate-mates in the fat.** Push the scallion or onion rings into the pooling fat at the pan's edge and let them soften and brown. Garlic coins 30 seconds a side in the same fat until blond at the edges — the sweetest version of raw garlic, and half the table will eat these like chips. Checkpoint — the vegetables should be fat-glossed and softened, not crisped; they are condiments in progress, pulled to a side plate as each batch finishes.
  3. **The scissors are the clock.** At 12–15 minutes the slabs are deeply golden. Cut each slab crosswise with kitchen scissors directly on the pan into bite pieces (2–3 cm), toss the pieces once in the hot fat for a final 60–90 seconds to crisp the cut faces, and pull to the platter. The cut-and-toss is not a shortcut — the fresh faces take the crisping that makes classic samgyeopsal. Checkpoint — a test piece straight from the pan, dunked in gireumjang: shatter-crisp edge, yielding fat layer, tender meat layer, then the dip's sesame-salt. The three-layer bite is the doneness signal — if the fat layer is still bouncy and translucent, the slab needed more rendering; there is no such thing as "too long" here short of burning.
  4. **The ssam sequence, the whole point.** Leaf in the palm, a thin swipe of ssamjang (#SA01), a piece of hot pork, a grilled garlic coin, a chili slice or a strand of grilled scallion, fold, one bite. Between ssam: rice, kimchi, the gireumjang for the plain pieces. The table's rhythm belongs to the pan — as one batch leaves, the next slabs go on, and the tongs rotate. Checkpoint — the assembly test: the fold closes without tearing and delivers everything in one bite. A ssam that needs two bites was overloaded, and the guilty party does the next batch's tongs duty.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Samgyeopsal is a buy-and-grill record; the belly keeps 2 days refrigerated. Leftover cooked pieces keep 3 days and are the canonical kimchi fried rice meat — dice, fry hard in a spoon of their own reserved fat, kimchi and day-old rice in, sesame over. - Variations: the gochujang-dressed fork (#M11); dwaeji bulgogi (#M13) is the thinly-sliced dressed cousin; kimchi grilled in the pork fat beside the slabs is the one addition the classic table allows without ceremony — and the one every regular orders.
