**Stream: heritage — the pan that makes tofu a banchan.** Dubu jorim takes the blandest protein on earth and makes it the bowl that disappears first from the vegetarian corner of the table: firm tofu, pan-seared into golden plates, then braised briefly in soy-garlic-sesame until every plate wears the glaze. The technique is the two-stage logic the potato record (#B09) runs in reverse — sear first for structure, braise second for flavor — and it converts tofu skeptics more reliably than any deep fry.

Dubu Jorim Braised Tofu

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 15 minutes · Total: About 25 minutes · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The tofu

  • 400 g firm tofu, cut into 1 cm slabs, patted dry, salted lightly (about 2 g) and rested 10 minutes

The sear

  • 2 tbsp neutral oil (about 24 g)

The braise

  • 80 ml water
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml)
  • 1 tsp honey or sugar (about 5 g)
  • 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml), off heat
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g); a pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional
  • 1/2 Korean radish, sliced into thin half-moons (about 100 g), the traditional bed — optional but excellent

Instructions

  1. The sear: oil at medium-high, slabs in without touching, 3–4 minutes to deep gold (they release when ready — nudged early, they tear), flip once, 2–3 minutes more, out to a plate. Checkpoint — both faces evenly golden-brown with a firming surface — the crust is the sponge that will drink the braise; a pale slab stays bland no matter how good the sauce.
  2. Pour off the oil, braise liquids into the pan (water, soy, honey, garlic), radish bed if using, seared slabs back over. Simmer 4–5 minutes at medium-low, spooning the liquid over the slabs twice. Checkpoint — the liquid has reduced to a glossy shallow pool and the slabs' faces have gone from dry-gold to lacquered amber; the tofu, pressed thin before, has visibly drunk.
  3. Sesame oil off the heat, sesame and scallion over. Checkpoint — the eating test: a slab on rice — savory-sweet glaze, the crust's chew, the soft interior, and the radish (if used) sweet enough to surprise. It should taste like the banchan board's quiet centerpiece, which at a table full of grilled meat is exactly what it is.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2 days refrigerated; reheat in the pan 2 minutes with a splash of water. Cold glazed slabs in a lunchbox with rice are a Korean cafeteria staple by right. - Variations: gochugaru-dusted braise for the red bowl; the same braise over mushrooms and tofu together; a slice of cheese melted on the hot slab is the school-lunch fork — fusion, labeled, beloved.
