**Stream: contemporary — the fusion seat, labeled honestly.** Doenjang and butter is the modern Korean bistro's signature move, and scallops are its best argument: the paste's fermented depth against sweet shellfish richness, with butter carrying both. Nothing about this record claims a lineage — it is exactly what its label says, a contemporary build using the pantry's oldest paste — and it is on the site because it is delicious and because it teaches the doenjang-and-fat principle better than any stew does.

- **Buy dry-packed scallops and dry them further.** Wet scallops (soaked in phosphate solution) never sear — they leach white liquid into the pan and steam in it. Dry-packed, patted down, and left air-drying on paper towel for 10 minutes while the grill heats: that is the sear's whole foundation. - **Whisk the baste warm:** butter melted, doenjang worked in until smooth (a small bowl and a fork, one minute), honey, garlic, sesame oil. It should taste intensely savory-salty-buttery — it is a glaze in search of sweetness, which is what the scallop supplies. - Fire at medium-high with a flat cast-iron surface or grate bars wiped with oil — scallops need full contact, not grates with gaps.

Doenjang-Butter Grilled Scallops

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 8 minutes · Total: About 20 minutes · Serves 3–4 (makes 12)

Ingredients

The scallops

  • 12 large sea scallops (about 500 g), dry-packed (no phosphate solution — see Note), patted bone-dry

The baste

  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (about 30 g), melted
  • 1.5 tsp doenjang (about 9 g)
  • 1 tsp honey (about 4 g)
  • 1 clove garlic (about 4 g), grated
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (5 ml)

The finish and table

  • Flaky salt (about 2 g), sliced scallion (about 10 g), toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • Thinly sliced cucumber or perilla, rice for the table

Instructions

  1. Sear dry scallops, undisturbed, 90 seconds — no baste yet, because butter burns where the paste would scorch. The sear happens in plain fat and the flavor comes after. Checkpoint — lift one: a deep golden-brown crust on the bottom face. No crust, back down — and if the pan was smoking hard, it ran a notch hot for butter's tolerance.
  2. Flip, and baste the seared faces now — brush or spoon the doenjang-butter over each scallop, 60–90 seconds more, off the fire while the centers still carry the faintest translucence at the very core (carryover finishes them on the platter). Checkpoint — the basted tops should sit glossy and foamy-gold. A test scallop cut in half: opaque to within a few millimeters of the center, buttery, just springy. Fully opaque-through at the pan means slightly over — edible, but the next batch pulls 15 seconds sooner.
  3. Flaky salt, scallion, sesame; serve immediately, off the shared platter, one or two per diner with rice beside. Checkpoint — eating test: crust, then the sweet scallop burst, then doenjang's savory depth arriving on the butter — the three arriving in that order is the build working. Salty-only means the baste ran heavy on paste; a teaspoon, not a tablespoon, is the ratio.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Scallops are cook-and-eat; raw ones keep 24 hours on ice in the fridge, no marinade. The baste keeps a week refrigerated and improves. - Variations: a drop of gochujang into the baste for heat; the same baste on king oyster mushroom slices for the table's vegetarians; stirred into rice at the end — the leftover baste's best job.
