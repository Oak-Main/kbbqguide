Pajeori Korean Scallion Salad

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 as the table's salad

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the grill's counterweight.** Pajeori is the scallion salad that lands beside the meat at every Korean grill table: big-cut scallions tossed in a bright gochugaru-soy-sesame dressing, slightly pickled by their own contact with salt, crunchy and sharp against the richest plates. It is the record the M-list cites more than any other fresh bowl — the galbi records (#M03, #M04) and the bulgogi (#M01) all reach for it — because scallion against beef fat is one of the cuisine's oldest and best pairings.

Pajeori Korean Scallion Salad

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes · Serves 4 as the table's salad

Ingredients

The scallions

  • 3 large bundles scallions (about 300 g), cut into 5 cm lengths, the whites halved lengthwise into thick ribbons

The dressing

  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (about 6 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp honey or sugar (about 5 g)
  • 1 tsp rice vinegar (5 ml), optional but the table's brightness
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)
  • 1 clove garlic (about 4 g), grated, optional

Instructions

  1. The cut scallions in the bowl; the dressing poured over — gochugaru, soy, honey, vinegar, sesame oil — and tossed with your hands for a full minute, squeezing the dressing gently through the ribbons as you turn them. Checkpoint — the scallions have just begun to glisten and bend — the salt starting its fast half-pickle — and every ribbon wears red-flecked gloss. It should taste sharp, savory, faintly sweet, with a slow gochugaru hum.
  2. Sesame seeds over, one more toss, and the bowl goes to the table beside the meat. Checkpoint — the eating test: a pinch of pajeori and a piece of galbi (#M03) in the same bite — the scallion's snap and its clean heat cutting the marinated beef's sweetness. The bowl should empty at the pace of the meat; pajeori leftover at meal's end means the table missed its purpose.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Eats best inside 30 minutes; a leftover bowl is tomorrow's noodle or fried-rice topping, still honest. - Variations: thin-sliced onion ribbons folded through; a pinch of black pepper for the beef records; the same dressing on shaved radish runs toward #B08's lane.
