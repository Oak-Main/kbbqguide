**Stream: restaurant.** The soy fork of pork bulgogi: same thin slices, same fast tabletop grill, no gochujang in sight. Ganjang bulgogi is the version for the table that wants the sweet-savory register without the heat — kids' approval guaranteed, adults' silence guaranteed, because it disappears first at every mixed grill. It is also the best introduction to the genre: everything you learn here (the thin slice, the single layer, the flip at the weep) transfers directly to the spicy cousin (#M13).

Ganjang Pork Bulgogi

Prep: 15 minutes, plus 2–4 hours marinating · Cook: 10 minutes at the table · Total: About 25 minutes at the table (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The pork

  • 900 g pork shoulder or collar, sliced 4 mm against the grain

The marinade

  • 80 ml soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp honey or brown sugar (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 5 cloves garlic (about 20 g), grated
  • 1 thumb ginger (about 10 g), grated
  • 1/2 onion (about 75 g), grated
  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 150 g), grated, juice included here — this lane wants the juice
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper (about 2 g); 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), lettuce ssam, ssamjang (#SA01), kimchi (#B01), gyeran mari rolled omelet (#B11) for the kids' plate

Instructions

  1. Medium-high fire, single loose layer, sixty to ninety seconds undisturbed. The wet marinade wants a hiss, not a roar — if it boils gray in its own liquid, the pan is crowded; lift half the batch off and do two rounds. Checkpoint — the slices take color at the edges while the marinade caramelizes on the pan behind them — the smell is sweet soy and pork fat, and the pan should be building a glossy brown film, not a watery boil.
  2. Flip at the beading edge, 60–90 seconds more, pull while the centers still shine. Scrape the pan's caramelized juice into a small bowl as you go — over rice is where it lands. Checkpoint — test piece: sweet-savory edge, juicy interior, no heat anywhere — the register should be round and gentle, with the pear as a perfume rather than a flavor.
  3. Platter, sesame over; the bowl route (rice, juice, slices) and the ssam route both open. Checkpoint — the rice bowl test: the juice should be glossy brown and spoonable, milder than the marinade tasted raw — if it is syrupy-sweet, pull the honey back a tablespoon next batch.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Marinated pork keeps 24 hours. Cooked, 3 days — leftover ganjang bulgogi over cold rice with a fried egg and a spoon of the reserved juice is the lunchbox move. - Variations: the spicy fork (#M13); add gochugaru by the half-teaspoon to walk this lane toward the heat gently; chicken thigh takes the same marinade (#M16).
