Radish Sprout and Perilla Salad

Korean · Easy · Serves 4 as banchan

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Stream: heritage — the two textures bowl.** Radish sprouts (dorumuk in Korean) and shredded perilla make this fresh list's most Korean-tasting salad: the sprouts' peppery crunch and the perilla's anise depth in a light soy-sesame dressing. It is the bowl that tastes most like a Korean market's sprout stall, and the natural companion to the delicate white-meat records — the fish (#SF09) and the chicken lanes (#M16).

The greens

The dressing

- **Roll-and-shred the perilla** — stack the leaves, roll them into a tight cigar, and slice across into ribbons. Two minutes of knife work that distributes the leaf's strong flavor through every bite instead of leaving it in whole-leaf punches. - Trim the sprouts' root tangle with one scissor cut and wash them hard — sprouts grow dense and hold grit at the root end.

Radish Sprout and Perilla Salad

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 15 minutes, serve within the hour · Serves 4 as banchan

Ingredients

The greens

  • 150 g radish sprouts (dorumuk), root ends trimmed, washed and spun dry
  • 10 perilla leaves (about 15 g), rolled and shredded into ribbons

The dressing

  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp honey (about 5 g)
  • 1 tsp rice vinegar (5 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g)
  • Black pepper; a pinch of gochugaru (about 1 g), optional
  • 1 scallion (about 15 g), sliced

Instructions

  1. The dressing whisked light, the tomato bowl's (#F07) register: soy present, sesame forward, nothing loud. Checkpoint — tastes gentle and nutty; both greens run assertive on their own, and the dressing referees.
  2. Sprouts and perilla ribbons folded through with spoons — the sprouts' fine stems want a lifting turn, not a pressing toss — sesame and scallion over. Checkpoint — the eating test: crunch of sprout, then the perilla's herbal wave arriving behind it, the sesame binding the two — a bowl that tastes green in three registers at once. Beside the yellow croaker (#SF09), it is the traditional sharp counter to the cured fish's depth.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Eats within the hour. Sprouts keep 2 days in paper towel in the fridge; perilla, 3 days. - Variations: matchstick pear folded in (#F06's lane); the sprouts blanched ten seconds for the soft fork; a gochugaru-heavier dressing for the drinking table.
